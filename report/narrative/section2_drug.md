The drug's laboratory potency, functional activity in cells and selectivity were measured before entry for {{section2.drug.measured|pct0}} of programs, nearly all small molecules ({{section2.drug.measured_by_modality.small_molecule.share|pct0}} of them); ChEMBL holds almost no measurements for antibodies or other proteins. Measured drugs were usually potent ({{section2.drug.potent_of_measured|pct0}} at 100 nM or better).

Among measured drugs, those at 100 nM or better reached approval {{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.rs|x2}} times as often as the {{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.n_without|int}} weaker ones ({{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.rs_hi|x2}}), and potency still helps at Phase 3 once the disease evidence is held fixed ({{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_3.rr|x2}}×). Selectivity shows no association ({{section2.rs.assay_selective.rs|x2}}×), and a functional readout in cells, if anything, goes with worse odds ({{section2.rs.assay_cell_functional.rs|x2}}×). The fair reading is that a drug well short of 100 nM at its target rarely succeeds, not that more potency helps; and because promising drugs attract more papers, a measurement is partly a marker of a well-studied program.
