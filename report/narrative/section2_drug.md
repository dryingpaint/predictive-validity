The drug's own laboratory pharmacology is measured for small molecules only: the bioactivity database holds no potency measurements for antibodies, and the affinities published for them are almost all 100 nM or better, so a threshold would not separate them. Of {{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.eligible|int}} US-developed small-molecule entries, {{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.exposed|int}} had a published potency of 100 nM or better at the primary target before entry. They reached approval {{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.rs_rs|x2}} times as often ({{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.rs_rs_hi|x2}}; {{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_10nm.rs_rs|x2}}× at 10 nM or better), but did not advance from Phase 2 more often with disease area, modality, novelty and precedent held fixed ({{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.s4_rr|x2}}×, {{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.s4_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.potent_100nm.s4_hi|x2}}), and outside oncology potency adds nothing once human genetics and drug-likeness are known ({{evidence_analysis.N3.rr|x2}}×). At least 10-fold selectivity over the off-targets measured ({{evidence_analysis.lines.selective.rs_rs|x2}}×), drug-like properties ({{evidence_analysis.lines.drug_like.rs_rs|x2}}×) and, in neurology and psychiatry, properties that let a molecule cross into the brain ({{evidence_analysis.lines.brain_penetrant.rs_rs|x2}}×) show no association with approval.

The fair reading is that a drug well short of 100 nM at its target rarely succeeds, not that more potency helps; and because promising drugs attract more papers, a measurement is partly a marker of a well-studied program. The category as a whole, among small molecules whose structure is known, shows nothing ({{evidence_analysis.lines.cat_molecule.rs_rs|x2}}×): nearly all of them are drug-like, so the category is mostly drug-likeness.
