The lines above concern the target and the disease. The drug-pharmacology lines concern the molecule: how strongly it acts on its primary target in laboratory assays (the concentration giving half its maximal effect, or its binding affinity), whether that was measured as a functional response in living cells, and how much more strongly it acts on its target than on any other human protein tested. Each measurement counts from the year of the paper, patent or dataset that reported it, as of the year before phase entry; the definitions are in the appendix.

Coverage is thin and lopsided. {{section2.drug.measured|pct0}} of the programs scored had any such measurement at their primary target before a phase entry: {{section2.drug.measured_by_modality.small_molecule.share|pct0}} of small-molecule programs and {{section2.drug.measured_by_modality.peptide.share|pct0}} of peptide programs, but {{section2.drug.measured_by_modality.monoclonal_antibody.share|pct0}} of antibody and {{section2.drug.measured_by_modality.protein.share|pct0}} of other protein programs, because ChEMBL's curated measurements come from medicinal chemistry papers and patents about small molecules. Where a drug was measured, it was usually potent: the median best potency was {{section2.drug.best_median|x2}} on the negative-log scale ({{section2.drug.best_median_nm|x2}} nM), and {{section2.drug.potent_of_measured|pct0}} of measured programs reached 100 nM or better. Off-target measurements existed for {{section2.drug.off_measured_of_measured|pct0}} of measured programs, and {{section2.drug.selective_of_off_measured|pct0}} of those were at least 100-fold selective.

A line carried by fewer than {{section2.min_programs_rs|int}} programs is not estimated; all five drug lines clear that bar. Having a measurement at all says nothing about success ({{section2.rs.assay_measured.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs.assay_measured.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.assay_measured.rs_hi|x2}}). Potency does, a little, once the comparison is kept among drugs that were measured: those at 100 nM or better reached approval {{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.rs|x2}} times as often as the {{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.n_without|int}} measured programs whose drug was weaker ({{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.rs_hi|x2}}); against all programs, measured or not, the ratio is {{section2.rs.assay_potent.rs|x2}}× and the interval spans 1. In the joint model, with the disease evidence, disease area, modality, novelty and era held fixed, a potent drug advanced more often at every phase ({{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_1.rr|x2}} at Phase 1, {{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_1.rr_lo|x2}}–{{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_1.rr_hi|x2}}; {{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_2.rr|x2}} at Phase 2, {{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_2.rr_lo|x2}}–{{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_2.rr_hi|x2}}; {{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_3.rr|x2}} at Phase 3, {{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_3.rr_lo|x2}}–{{section2.joint_rr.assay_potent_3.rr_hi|x2}}). Raising the bar to 10 nM separates measured programs less sharply ({{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_very_potent.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_very_potent.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_very_potent.rs_hi|x2}}). Selectivity of 100-fold or more shows no association ({{section2.rs.assay_selective.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs.assay_selective.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.assay_selective.rs_hi|x2}}), and neither does it at any phase in the joint model. Functional potency measured in cells goes the wrong way ({{section2.rs.assay_cell_functional.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs.assay_cell_functional.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.assay_cell_functional.rs_hi|x2}}; {{section2.joint_rr.assay_cell_functional_1.rr|x2}} at Phase 1 in the joint model, {{section2.joint_rr.assay_cell_functional_1.rr_lo|x2}}–{{section2.joint_rr.assay_cell_functional_1.rr_hi|x2}}), a pattern these data cannot explain; a cell-based readout is no sign that a program will do better. Counting every measurement published by 2026 instead of before entry gives {{section2.rs_untimed.assay_potent_untimed.rs|x2}}× for potency against all programs.

These lines measure the molecule, not whether its target matters in the disease, and they come with a bias of their own: drugs that look promising attract more papers, and potency is published mostly for small molecules from medicinal chemistry programs, so a measurement is partly a marker of a well-studied, novel-chemistry program. Weak measured drugs are a small group of {{section2.rs_drug_measured.assay_potent.n_without|int}} programs, and the clearest reading is that a drug well short of 100 nM at its intended target rarely reaches approval, not that more potency beyond that point helps.
