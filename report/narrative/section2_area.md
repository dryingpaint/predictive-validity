Human genetic evidence predicts approval in every area group with enough programs to measure it, and its effect does not differ measurably between them (p = {{section2.area.tests.genetic_any.p|x2}} for the difference at Phase 2). It is largest in cardiometabolic and endocrine diseases ({{section2.area.rs.cardiometabolic_genetic_any.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.area.rs.cardiometabolic_genetic_any.loa_with|pct0}} against {{section2.area.rs.cardiometabolic_genetic_any.loa_without|pct0}}) and neuroscience ({{section2.area.rs.neuroscience_genetic_any.rs|x2}}×), smaller in oncology ({{section2.area.rs.oncology_genetic_any.rs|x2}}×), and least clear in immunology and inflammation ({{section2.area.rs.immunology_genetic_any.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.area.rs.immunology_genetic_any.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.area.rs.immunology_genetic_any.rs_hi|x2}}), where much of the evidence is GWAS. Hematology and the other areas have too few genetically supported programs to estimate. Leaving out any one group keeps the pooled relative success between {{section2.area.loo_min.rs|x2}} and {{section2.area.loo_max|x2}}.

Somatic drivers give {{section2.area.rs.oncology_somatic.rs|x2}}× in oncology. Mouse models help in cardiometabolic disease ({{section2.area.rs.cardiometabolic_animal_model.rs|x2}}×) and immunology ({{section2.area.rs.immunology_animal_model.rs|x2}}×) but not in oncology ({{section2.area.rs.oncology_animal_model.rs|x2}}×). Expression in the disease is the one line whose effect differs between groups (p = {{section2.area.tests.rna_expression.p|x2}}), going the wrong way in immunology ({{section2.area.rs.immunology_rna_expression.rs|x2}}×); with several lines and groups tested, this is a lead, not a finding.
