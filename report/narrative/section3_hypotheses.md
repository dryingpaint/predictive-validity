Two of the seven hypotheses hold after correction for multiple testing.

* **H1, H2 (supported).** Outside oncology, Phase 2 programs advanced within four years at {{section3.h1.g0.ppv|pct0}} with no germline genetic evidence on the target ({{section3.h1.g0.n|int}} programs), {{section3.h1.g1.ppv|pct0}} with evidence only for unrelated diseases ({{section3.h1.g1.n|int}}), {{section3.h1.g2.ppv|pct0}} with evidence for a related disease ({{section3.h1.g2.n|int}}) and {{section3.h1.g3.ppv|pct0}} with evidence for the disease itself ({{section3.h1.g3.n|int}}); only the last differs from none once disease area, modality, novelty, period and target precedent are accounted for. Among the {{section3.h2.informative_targets|int}} targets developed in several diseases with and without genetic support and with different outcomes ({{section3.h2.programs|int}} programs), the diseases with support advanced more often (odds ratio {{section3.h2.or|x2}}, {{section3.h2.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h2.hi|x2}}): the signal is not only that some targets are better than others.
* **H3 (not supported).** Open Targets' direction-of-effect fields follow each variant's predicted consequence, so a few truncating ClinVar variants make gain-of-function disease genes such as PCSK9, MYH7 and TTR read as loss of function, and inhibitors of them as discordant. The test is reported as registered; the direction analysis below replaces those fields.
* **H4 (supported in the registered analysis; not robust).** In trials with registry-posted results, genetic support lowered the chance of missing the primary endpoint most on proximal endpoints (risk ratio {{section3.h4.fdaaa.g3_at_proximal.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h4.fdaaa.g3_at_proximal.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h4.fdaaa.g3_at_proximal.hi|x2}}), and the effect weakened by a factor of {{section3.h4.fdaaa.interaction_per_step.rr|x2}} per step towards distal endpoints (p = {{section3.h4.fdaaa.interaction_per_step.p|x3}}; {{section3.holm.H4|x3}} after Holm correction). The registered test survives correction, but the pattern does not hold across all readable trials ({{section3.h4.all_readable.interaction_per_step.rr|x2}}, p = {{section3.h4.all_readable.interaction_per_step.p|x3}}) or with mixed results counted as met ({{section3.h4.fdaaa_mixed_as_met.interaction_per_step.rr|x2}}).
* **H5 (not supported).** Genetic support lowered efficacy stops (risk ratio {{section3.h5.rr_f_efficacy.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h5.rr_f_efficacy.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h5.rr_f_efficacy.hi|x2}}), but business and operational stops fell by a similar factor (ratio of the two {{section3.h5.ratio_f_bizops_vs_f_efficacy.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h5.ratio_f_bizops_vs_f_efficacy.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h5.ratio_f_bizops_vs_f_efficacy.hi|x2}}), so the data cannot say that it acts on efficacy alone. Safety stops were no rarer ({{section3.controls.safety_failure_g3.rr|x2}}, {{section3.controls.safety_failure_g3.lo|x2}}–{{section3.controls.safety_failure_g3.hi|x2}}), nor were stops for pharmacokinetic, formulation or operational reasons, the plan's negative control ({{section3.controls.pkops_failure_g3.rr|x2}}, {{section3.controls.pkops_failure_g3.lo|x2}}–{{section3.controls.pkops_failure_g3.hi|x2}}).
* **H6 (not supported).** Targets that are pan-essential, intolerant of loss of function, or lethal when knocked out in mice did not stop for safety more often ({{section3.h6.safety.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h6.safety.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h6.safety.hi|x2}}). Safety is the recorded reason for fewer than 2% of programs, so registries see little of it.
* **H7 (not supported).** In oncology, a somatic driver whose direction matches the drug's (an activated oncogene and an inhibitor) did not raise the chance of advancing ({{section3.h7.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h7.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h7.hi|x2}}).

**Checks on H1.** Swapping each program's disease for another program's in the same disease area, {{section3.permutation.n_permutations|int}} times, gives a median G3 risk ratio of {{section3.permutation.null_median|x2}} against the observed {{section3.permutation.observed|x2}} (95th percentile {{section3.permutation.null_95|x2}}; p = {{section3.permutation.p_one_sided|x3}}). Part of the contrast with G0 therefore reflects whether a target has been studied genetically at all; matching the disease adds to it. At Phase 1, where efficacy is rarely tested, the same contrast is {{section3.controls.phase1_g3.rr|x2}} ({{section3.controls.phase1_g3.lo|x2}}–{{section3.controls.phase1_g3.hi|x2}}). Against all other programs rather than G0, genetic support gives {{section3.map.C2_COM.g3.rr|x2}} outside oncology, and {{section3.beyond_precedent.COM.adjusted_for_b1.rr|x2}} ({{section3.beyond_precedent.COM.adjusted_for_b1.lo|x2}}–{{section3.beyond_precedent.COM.adjusted_for_b1.hi|x2}}) after adjusting for an approved drug on the target for a related disease; on targets with no approved drug at all, genetic support gives {{section3.beyond_precedent.C2.novel_targets.rr|x2}} ({{section3.beyond_precedent.C2.novel_targets.lo|x2}}–{{section3.beyond_precedent.C2.novel_targets.hi|x2}}, all disease areas). Leaving out the most influential target set ({{section3.influence.most_influential}}) gives {{section3.influence.rr_without|x2}}. Leaving out each of the {{section3.influence.classes|int}} largest target classes in turn gives {{section3.influence.class_min.rr|x2}} to {{section3.influence.class_max|x2}}; the lowest, without the "{{section3.influence.lowest_class}}" class, has an interval of {{section3.influence.class_min.lo|x2}}–{{section3.influence.class_min.hi|x2}}, so the estimate leans on that class.

**Who is in the cohort.** The cohort is Phase 2 programs with a disease and a single resolved human protein target. Left out are {{section3.excluded.no_disease.programs|int}} programs with no disease (healthy-volunteer, basket or symptom trials; {{section3.excluded.no_disease.s4|pct0}} advanced) and {{section3.excluded.no_protein_target.programs|int}} without a single human protein target ({{section3.excluded.no_protein_target.s4|pct0}}), so the cohort's base rate is higher than Section 1's.
