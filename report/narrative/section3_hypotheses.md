One of the seven registered hypotheses holds after correction for multiple testing: genetics for the disease predicts advancing within a target, across the {{section3.h2.informative_targets|int}} targets developed in several diseases (odds ratio {{section3.h2.or|x2}}, {{section3.h2.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h2.hi|x2}}), so the signal is not only that some targets are better than others. Outside oncology, genetics for the disease goes with advancing (risk ratio {{section3.h1.g3.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h1.g3.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h1.g3.hi|x2}}; only evidence for the disease itself, not a related or unrelated one, differs from none) but does not survive the correction (Holm p = {{section3.holm.H1|x2}}).

The other five are not supported. Open Targets' direction-of-effect fields could not test direction (they mislabel gain-of-function genes such as PCSK9 and MYH7; the appendix gives the replacement analysis). Genetic support lowered efficacy stops ({{section3.h5.rr_f_efficacy.rr|x2}}) but business stops by about as much. Its effect did not clearly weaken from proximal to distal endpoints. Target liabilities did not predict safety stops, which are under 2% of programs. A somatic driver matching the drug went with advancing ({{section3.h7.rr|x2}}) but not beyond chance across seven tests.
