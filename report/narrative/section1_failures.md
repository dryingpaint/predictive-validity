The registry rarely says why a program stopped. In Phase 1, {{section1.failure_modes.family_share.1.unexplained|pct0}} of stopped programs left neither a stated reason nor a result, which is expected: Phase 1 trials seldom post results and companies seldom explain pausing early assets. In Phase 2 and Phase 3 the share without a reason falls to {{section1.failure_modes.family_share.2.unexplained|pct0}} and {{section1.failure_modes.family_share.3.unexplained|pct0}}. Efficacy is the recorded reason for {{section1.failure_modes.biology.2.efficacy|pct0}} of programs that stopped in Phase 2 and {{section1.failure_modes.biology.3.efficacy|pct0}} of those that stopped in Phase 3: the sponsor said so, or the posted or published primary endpoint was missed. Safety accounts for {{section1.failure_modes.biology.2.safety|pct0}} and {{section1.failure_modes.biology.3.safety|pct0}}: among Phase 2 and Phase 3 stops with an efficacy or safety reason, safety is {{section1.failure_modes.biology.2.safety_of_efficacy_or_safety|pct0}} and {{section1.failure_modes.biology.3.safety_of_efficacy_or_safety|pct0}}, against about 32% in Harrison (2016), whose 2013–2015 failures with a disclosed reason were 24% safety and 52% efficacy. Either registry records under-report safety stops, or company disclosures over-represent them.

At the level of individual trials, the {{section1.failure_modes.terminations.n|int}} industry trials started in 2008–2019 that stopped early or were withdrawn with a reason give: an explicit business, portfolio or competitive reason {{section1.failure_modes.terminations.business|pct0}}, a bare "sponsor decision" {{section1.failure_modes.terminations.sponsor_decision|pct0}}, efficacy {{section1.failure_modes.terminations.efficacy|pct0}}, enrollment or operations {{section1.failure_modes.terminations.enrollment_operational|pct0}} and safety {{section1.failure_modes.terminations.safety|pct0}}. The draft's single 44% "commercial or strategic" category mixed these two kinds of statement; a "sponsor decision" with no reason may or may not hide an efficacy result, and the record cannot tell. No study has measured how often a stated business reason hides an efficacy or safety failure. Where a registry gives a reason it mostly agrees with the trial's publication (53 of 57 in [Alturki et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2016.08.011)); the usual gap is a missing reason. Analysts who also read company filings, press releases and regulator records attribute 57% of drug failures in pivotal trials to efficacy, 17% to safety and 22% to commercial reasons ([Hwang et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.6008)), so the efficacy and safety shares here are a floor.

Finally, {{section1.failure_modes.family_share.3.positive_readout|pct0}} of Phase 3 programs that stopped short of FDA approval had met their primary endpoint. In a sample we read, most of them were approved outside the US (Japan, China, Korea, Russia) or received a complete response letter from the FDA: a positive Phase 3 readout is necessary but not sufficient for US approval.
