Approval rises with the strength of genetic support, from {{section2.dose.none.loa|pct0}} with none to {{section2.dose.high.loa|pct0}} in the top third. It also depends on how close the evidence lies to the program's disease: outside oncology, Phase 2 programs advanced at {{section3.h1.g0.ppv|pct0}} with no germline genetics on the target, {{section3.h1.g1.ppv|pct0}} with evidence only for unrelated diseases, {{section3.h1.g2.ppv|pct0}} for a related disease and {{section3.h1.g3.ppv|pct0}} for the disease itself, and the same target does better in the diseases its genetics points to (odds ratio {{section3.h2.or|x2}}).

Beyond that, sharper versions do not help. Among programs with germline genetics, strong GWAS evidence ({{section3.refinements.gwas_strong.rr|x2}}), Mendelian evidence ({{section3.refinements.mendelian_nonhereditary.rr|x2}}) and genetics pointing the drug's way ({{section3.refinements.mechanism_concordant.rr|x2}}) did no better than the rest; nor did a close phenotype match ({{section3.refinements.animal_close.rr|x2}}) or a mouse model pointing the drug's way ({{section3.refinements.animal_direction.rr|x2}}) among programs with a mouse model, or a driver matching the drug among oncology programs with a driver ({{section3.refinements.somatic_matching.rr|x2}}). Direction could not be tested properly: of the Phase 2 programs with genetic support, {{section3.mechanism.levels.concordant.programs|int}} acted in the direction the genetics favoured and only {{section3.mechanism.levels.discordant.programs|int}} against it, because sponsors almost never develop against an established mechanism.
