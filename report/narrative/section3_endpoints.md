Across {{section3.trials.n|int}} randomised industry efficacy trials, {{section3.trials.not_met|pct0}} missed their governing primary endpoint. Regulatory surrogates were missed least ({{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.not_met|pct0}}); outside oncology, exploratory biomarkers ({{section3.endpoints.B2_exploratory_biomarker_all.not_met|pct0}}) and clinician-rated scales ({{section3.endpoints.B5_clinician_scale_all.not_met|pct0}}) were missed most, and oncology's response and survival endpoints as often.

Genetic support for the disease did not measurably change the chance of missing any class of endpoint. For regulatory surrogates the risk ratio is {{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_fdaaa.rr|x2}} ({{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_fdaaa.lo|x2}}–{{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_fdaaa.hi|x2}}) in trials with registry-posted results since FDAAA and {{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.rr|x2}} ({{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.lo|x2}}–{{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.hi|x2}}) across all readable trials, including those read from papers; clinician-rated scales ({{section3.endpoints.B5_clinician_scale_all.rr|x2}}), objective physiology ({{section3.endpoints.B4_objective_physiological_all.rr|x2}}), disease events ({{section3.endpoints.B7_disease_events_all.rr|x2}}) and oncology's response and progression endpoints all have intervals spanning 1. So these trials cannot tie the advantage genetically supported programs show in advancing to any one kind of endpoint.
