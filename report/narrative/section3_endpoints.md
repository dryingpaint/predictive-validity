Across {{section3.trials.n|int}} randomised industry efficacy trials, {{section3.trials.not_met|pct0}} missed their governing primary endpoint. Regulatory surrogates were missed least ({{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.not_met|pct0}}); outside oncology, exploratory biomarkers ({{section3.endpoints.B2_exploratory_biomarker_all.not_met|pct0}}) and hard outcomes ({{section3.endpoints.B8_hard_outcome_all.not_met|pct0}}) were missed most, and oncology's response and survival endpoints as often.

Genetic support for the disease lowered the chance of missing a regulatory surrogate most clearly in trials with registry-posted results since FDAAA (risk ratio {{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_fdaaa.rr|x2}}, {{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_fdaaa.lo|x2}}–{{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_fdaaa.hi|x2}}); across all readable trials, including those read from papers, the effect is smaller ({{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.rr|x2}}, {{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.lo|x2}}–{{section3.endpoints.B3_regulatory_surrogate_all.hi|x2}}). Clinician-rated scales ({{section3.endpoints.B5_clinician_scale_all.rr|x2}}), objective physiology ({{section3.endpoints.B4_objective_physiological_all.rr|x2}}) and disease events ({{section3.endpoints.B7_disease_events_all.rr|x2}}) point the same way with intervals reaching 1, and oncology's response and progression endpoints show no benefit. Genetic support predicts most reliably on the measures nearest its biology, often the trait through which the genetics was found (LDL cholesterol, glycaemia), and less clearly on what patients feel or how often events happen.
