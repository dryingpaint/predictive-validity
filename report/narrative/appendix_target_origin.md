### How target origins were labelled and tested

**Labelling.** Each target–disease pair is a drug's primary target gene and the program's disease, dated by the pair's first Phase 2 or 3 entry; {{target_origin.origins.labelled|int}} pairs were first tested in 2008–2019. For each, PubMed and Europe PMC were searched for records naming the gene and the disease and published before the entry year. An LLM read the earliest and latest of them, and could search the web under the same date rule, and named what first proposed the target for the disease, from the kinds of evidence of the shared list (Definitions in the Appendix detail tab), with a quote and a source dated before entry. It graded its answer as stated by the source, inferred from a pattern of sources, or unsettled. Two checker models then verified each graded label: that the quote is in the source, that the source is dated before entry, and that the quote supports the origin. Dated facts such as a drug's approval for another disease were checked in code against the FDA records, not by a model. A label either checker rejected became unclear; one the two checkers disagreed on was disputed, and was settled by one further reading of the cited text alone, without search. The result: {{target_origin.origins.settled|int}} pairs with a settled origin, {{target_origin.origins.unclear|int}} unclear, {{target_origin.origins.disputed|int}} still disputed, and {{target_origin.origins.not_reached|int}} for which labelling never returned a valid answer. Before the full run, two sets of pairs were labelled again by independent blind readings from the same records; agreement on the origin was Cohen's κ {{target_origin.agreement.set_b.kappa|x2}} and {{target_origin.agreement.set_a.kappa|x2}} ({{target_origin.agreement.set_b.pairs|int}} and {{target_origin.agreement.set_a.pairs|int}} pairs).

**The registered test (O1).** Registered before it was run: among Phase 2 and 3 entries of 2008–2019 developed in the US ({{target_origin.cohort.entries|int}} entries, {{target_origin.cohort.events|int}} stops where the target was not causal), entries whose origin is an observational biomarker or a diseased-tissue change stop because the target was not causal more often than entries whose origin is human genetics; each of the two exposed origins is also compared alone. Only origins a stated or pattern label settles enter the comparison. The outcome is a stop in the "target not causal" failure mode of Section 1, per entry. The risk ratio comes from a modified Poisson model adjusted for disease area, modality, entry period, novelty and phase, with intervals clustered by the entry's set of primary targets, as for the other stop analyses. Phase 1 entries are left out, because no Phase 1 stop has the target's later record that the failure mode is read from.

**Which entries enter.** An entry's pairs are each of its primary targets with its disease. An entry takes an origin only when all its pairs share one: of {{target_origin.multi_target.entries|int}} entries with more than one primary target, {{target_origin.multi_target.single_origin|int}} do, {{target_origin.multi_target.mixed|int}} have pairs with different origins and {{target_origin.multi_target.unlabelled|int}} have a pair without a label; these are left out. Drugs acting on a pathogen have no human target and no pair ({{target_origin.status.pathogen|int}} entries); infectious-disease entries whose drug acts on a human target enter as any other ({{target_origin.infectious_host_target|int}}). Entries with no single human target ({{target_origin.status.no_target|int}}), whose target was already validated for the disease by an approved drug at entry ({{target_origin.status.validated_at_entry|int}}), or whose pairs were never labelled ({{target_origin.status.no_pair|int}} without a pair, {{target_origin.status.unlabelled|int}} first tested before 2008 or unlabelled) are left out too.

**An earlier drug as origin.** An entry whose target was first proposed by a drug approved for another disease is reported alone and in no comparison. That approval is itself the record the failure mode reads: a target that worked only in another disease is "not causal" for this one. Of its {{target_origin.circular_origins.earlier_drug_other_disease.entries|int}} entries, {{target_origin.circular_origins.earlier_drug_other_disease.events|int}} stopped as not causal, {{target_origin.circular_origins.earlier_drug_other_disease.events_by_fate_worked_elsewhere_only|int}} of them because the target had worked only elsewhere.

**Sensitivity and descriptive analyses.** The registered sensitivities add the unclear origins, and then also the disputed and unanswered ones, to each compared group in turn; keep Phase 2 entries only; and admit labels whose source was dated in or after the entry year ({{target_origin.any_date.labels_restored|int}} labels, moving {{target_origin.any_date.entries_moved|int}} entries). Two descriptive outcomes, not registered, replace the failure mode: a stop whose own sources call the target not causal ({{target_origin.descriptive_tests.events.not_causal_by_verdict|int}} stops, against {{target_origin.cohort.events|int}} by the failure mode), and a stop whose target never worked in this disease, whatever the failure mode ({{target_origin.descriptive_tests.events.target_never_worked_here|int}}). Risk ratios with 95% intervals; "of entries" counts the O1 groups' not-causal stops and entries.
