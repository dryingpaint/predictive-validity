Programs developed in the US, the closest match to BIO's FDA-focused Biomedtracker programs, advance from Phase 1 at {{section1.us_site.ph1_rate|pct}} (BIO {{bio.ph1}}), from Phase 2 at {{section1.us_site.ph2_rate|pct}} (BIO {{bio.ph2}}) and from Phase 3 to approval at {{section1.us_site.ph3_rate|pct}} (BIO {{bio.ph3}}). Phase 2 remains the steepest single drop. Across all registered industry programs the Phase 3 rate falls to {{section1.overall.ph3_rate|pct}}, because many programs run only outside the US end in approvals elsewhere, which this report does not count.

The likelihood of approval depends on choices a registry-based count has to make, and the table below shows how far each moves it. It is most sensitive to how long a program must be idle before it counts as suspended: {{section1.lag.12.loa|pct}} with a 12-month window and {{section1.lag.36.loa|pct}} with 36 months ({{section1.lag_months}} in the headline). If every shared trial (a healthy-volunteer study, a basket) counted as the start of every later indication of the drug, LOA would rise to {{section1.shared_entry.loa|pct}}, mostly by crediting indication expansions begun at Phase 2 with a Phase 1 success. Restricting to programs that entered a phase in 2015–2019, which have had at least six years to resolve, changes little ({{section1.mature_us.loa|pct}} for US-developed programs). Vaccines are left out of the headline figures: annual and variant strain updates are licensed as supplements to the parent product, so registry entities for updated formulations never reach an approval of their own (LOA with vaccines included: {{section1.with_vaccines.loa|pct}}).
