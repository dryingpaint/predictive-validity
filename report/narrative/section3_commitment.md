Advancing from Phase 2 is partly a sponsor's decision, so genetically supported programs might do better because sponsors back them harder, not because their biology is right. Two checks point mostly to the biology.

**It is not who runs them.** Genetically supported programs are about as common at the largest sponsors as at the smallest ({{section3.commitment.tiers.top20.genetics_share|pct0}} of Phase 2 programs at the 20 largest, {{section3.commitment.tiers.smaller.genetics_share|pct0}} at sponsors outside the top 100). Adding sponsor size to the model leaves the risk ratio of advancing at {{section3.commitment.models.size_tier.rr|x2}} ({{section3.commitment.models.reported.rr|x2}} as reported), and comparing each of the {{section3.commitment.own_baseline_sponsors|int}} largest sponsors only with itself leaves it at {{section3.commitment.models.own_baseline.rr|x2}}. The advantage holds in each size tier, from {{section3.commitment.tiers.top20.rr|x2}}× at the 20 largest to {{section3.commitment.tiers.smaller.rr|x2}}× at the smallest. At {{section3.commitment.within.higher|int}} of the {{section3.commitment.within.sponsors|int}} large sponsors with at least three programs of each kind, genetically supported programs advanced more often than the sponsor's others ({{section3.commitment.within.mean_with|pct0}} against {{section3.commitment.within.mean_without|pct0}} on average).

**Most of the advantage shows at the readout, which the sponsor does not choose.** For the {{section3.commitment.readout.n|int}} programs whose first randomised Phase 2 efficacy trial has a readable result, genetically supported programs met the primary endpoint more often ({{section3.commitment.readout.met_with|pct0}} against {{section3.commitment.readout.met_without|pct0}}; {{section3.commitment.readout.met_readout.rr|x2}}×, {{section3.commitment.readout.met_readout.lo|x2}}–{{section3.commitment.readout.met_readout.hi|x2}}). Given the readout, they advanced only somewhat more often, after a met endpoint ({{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_met.s4_with|pct0}} against {{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_met.s4_without|pct0}}; {{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_met.rr|x2}}×, {{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_met.lo|x2}}–{{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_met.hi|x2}}) and after a missed one ({{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_not_met.s4_with|pct0}} against {{section3.commitment.readout.advanced_if_not_met.s4_without|pct0}}), neither clearly. Some extra backing cannot be ruled out: genetic support lowered stops for sponsor decisions about as much as stops for lack of efficacy (ratio of the two effects {{section3.h5.ratio_f_bizops_vs_f_efficacy.rr|x2}}, {{section3.h5.ratio_f_bizops_vs_f_efficacy.lo|x2}}–{{section3.h5.ratio_f_bizops_vs_f_efficacy.hi|x2}}). Approval, which needs the FDA to accept the Phase 3 data, shows the advantage too ({{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs|x2}}×).
