### Tracing the stops the registry does not explain

Every program that entered Phase 2 or 3 from 2008 to 2025 and stopped was traced ({{stop_causes.traced.stops|int}} programs). The figures here are for the 2008–2019 entries of the Section 1 headline, with later entries compared below. For {{stop_causes.inferred.programs|int}} of their {{stop_causes.stops|int}} stops, the registry gives no reason, only "sponsor decision", an unspecified "other" reason, or an efficacy reason that the efficacy reading above found wrong. Each was traced. A model read the program's trials (records, posted results, linked papers) and structured signals: whether the drug went on in other diseases, whether its sponsor started any trial afterwards, whether drugs on the same target stopped at the same time. It then searched the web for company releases, SEC filings, pipeline updates, conference reports and trade news, with ClinicalTrials.gov excluded. It named a cause only where a source supported it, citing only pages its own searches returned, and separately gave a *likely* cause, its best inference from context, where no source says. Each step is recorded in the database (`preclin.analysis_method`, `preclin.stop_signal`, `preclin.stop_cause`), with every decision's inputs, searches and sources in `pv_evidence.llm_decision`.

A sourced cause was found for {{stop_causes.inferred.resolved|pct0}} of the traced programs ({{stop_causes.inferred.resolved_n|int}}), and a likely cause for {{stop_causes.inferred.likely_resolved|pct0}}. The biggest changes from the registry's picture:

* **Many quiet stops were not failures.** A positive readout that was not taken further in the disease (approval outside the US, a stalled partnership, a move to another formulation) was the sourced cause for {{stop_causes.inferred.cause.positive_not_advanced|int}} traced programs, and {{stop_causes.inferred.cause.development_continued|int}} had not stopped at all: development continued under another registry, region, name or combination. Section 1's suspension rule, which reads only ClinicalTrials.gov, counts these as stops, so its failure rates are somewhat high.
* **Efficacy is under-recorded.** Efficacy was the sourced cause of {{stop_causes.inferred.cause.efficacy|int}} traced programs, usually from published results or a company statement the registry never carried, raising efficacy from {{stop_causes.recorded.efficacy.share|pct0}} of all Phase 2/3 stops as registered to {{stop_causes.evidence.efficacy.share|pct0}} sourced ({{stop_causes.likely.efficacy.share|pct0}} counting likely causes).
* **Business reasons and company events are the rest.** Portfolio decisions account for {{stop_causes.likely.business_strategic.share|pct0}} of all stops counting likely causes, and acquisitions, mergers, lost funding or closures for {{stop_causes.likely.company_event.share|pct0}}. Safety stays rare ({{stop_causes.likely.safety.share|pct0}}).

**Over time.** Counting sourced and likely causes, and leaving out programs that turned out not to have stopped, efficacy or exposure was the cause of {{stop_causes.by_period.2008-2011.shown.efficacy.share|pct0}} of stops among programs entering in 2008–2011, {{stop_causes.by_period.2012-2015.shown.efficacy.share|pct0}} in 2012–2015 and {{stop_causes.by_period.2016-2019.shown.efficacy.share|pct0}} in 2016–2019; business, strategic and company events were {{stop_causes.by_period.2008-2011.shown.business_strategic.share|pct0}}, {{stop_causes.by_period.2012-2015.shown.business_strategic.share|pct0}} and {{stop_causes.by_period.2016-2019.shown.business_strategic.share|pct0}}, and a positive readout not yet advanced {{stop_causes.by_period.2008-2011.shown.positive_not_advanced.share|pct0}}, {{stop_causes.by_period.2012-2015.shown.positive_not_advanced.share|pct0}} and {{stop_causes.by_period.2016-2019.shown.positive_not_advanced.share|pct0}}. Among entries from 2015 to 2025 ({{stop_causes.recent.stops|int}} stops, many later programs still running), the shares are {{stop_causes.recent.shown.efficacy.share|pct0}} efficacy or exposure, {{stop_causes.recent.shown.business_strategic.share|pct0}} business and {{stop_causes.recent.shown.positive_not_advanced.share|pct0}} positive readouts.

**Phase 1.** Phase 1 stops were traced the same way ({{stop_causes.traced.phase1|int}} programs entering 2008–2025, {{stop_causes.phase1.stops|int}} of them in 2008–2019). As registered, {{stop_causes.phase1.recorded.unknown.share|pct0}} give no usable reason, since Phase 1 trials seldom post results and companies seldom explain pausing early assets. Tracing found a sourced cause for {{stop_causes.phase1.inferred.resolved|pct0}} of those and a likely cause for {{stop_causes.phase1.inferred.likely_resolved|pct0}}. Counting both, efficacy or exposure was the cause of {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.efficacy.share|pct0}} of Phase 1 stops, safety {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.safety.share|pct0}}, business, strategic and company events {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.business_strategic.share|pct0}}, operational {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.operational.share|pct0}} and a positive readout not yet advanced {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.positive_not_advanced.share|pct0}}; {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.unknown.share|pct0}} stay unknown.

**How far to trust it.** The same procedure was run on {{stop_causes.validation.n|int}} stops whose registry does state a reason, drawn at random across causes, with the reason removed and ClinicalTrials.gov blocked. It named a sourced cause for {{stop_causes.validation.resolved|pct0}} of them, and that cause matched the registry's for {{stop_causes.validation.cause_accuracy_resolved|pct0}} (κ = {{stop_causes.validation.cause_kappa_resolved|x2}}); the likely cause matched for {{stop_causes.validation.likely_accuracy|pct0}} (κ = {{stop_causes.validation.likely_kappa|x2}}). Accuracy differs by cause. Efficacy stops were recognised almost every time ({{stop_causes.validation.by_truth.efficacy.likely_correct|pct0}}), but only {{stop_causes.validation.precision_likely.efficacy.correct|pct0}} of the stops it called efficacy were efficacy stops in the registry: safety stops were often read as efficacy (companies announce both as an unfavourable benefit–risk), and business and operational stops were confused with each other ({{stop_causes.validation.by_truth.business_strategic.likely_correct|pct0}} and {{stop_causes.validation.by_truth.operational.likely_correct|pct0}} recognised). The efficacy share after tracing is therefore an upper estimate, and safety is likely under-counted. The registry's stated reason is itself an imperfect reference, since a stated business reason can hide a failed readout. Stops with a stated reason are probably also easier to trace, so accuracy on the unexplained stops is likely lower. One independent check holds: of programs traced to a company event, {{stop_causes.signals.company_event.sponsor_inactive|pct0}} had a sponsor that started no trial afterwards, against {{stop_causes.signals.efficacy.sponsor_inactive|pct0}} of those traced to efficacy.
