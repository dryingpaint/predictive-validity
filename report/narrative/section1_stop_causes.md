### Why the stops the registry does not explain happened

For {{stop_causes.inferred.programs|int}} of the {{stop_causes.stops|int}} Phase 2 and 3 stops among 2008–2019 entries, the registry gives no usable reason: none at all, only "sponsor decision", an unspecified "other" reason, or an efficacy reason that the reading of efficacy failures below found wrong. We classified each of these with an LLM that read the program's trials and searched the web for company releases, filings and news. It named a cause only where a source stated or showed it, and otherwise gave its best guess, the *likely* cause. The method and how well it does on stops whose reason is known are in the appendix.

A sourced cause was found for {{stop_causes.inferred.resolved|pct0}} of the traced programs ({{stop_causes.inferred.resolved_n|int}}), and a likely cause for {{stop_causes.inferred.likely_resolved|pct0}}. The biggest changes from the registry's picture:

* **Many quiet stops were not failures.** A positive readout that was not taken further in the disease (approval outside the US, a stalled partnership, a move to another formulation) was the sourced cause for {{stop_causes.inferred.cause.positive_not_advanced|int}} traced programs, and {{stop_causes.inferred.cause.not_stopped|int}} had not stopped at all: development continued under another registry, region, name or combination. Section 1's suspension rule, which reads only ClinicalTrials.gov, counts these as stops, so its failure rates are somewhat high.
* **Efficacy is under-recorded.** Efficacy or exposure was the sourced cause of {{stop_causes.inferred.cause.efficacy|int}} traced programs, usually from published results or a company statement the registry never carried, raising efficacy or exposure from {{stop_causes.recorded.efficacy.share|pct0}} of all Phase 2/3 stops as registered to {{stop_causes.evidence.efficacy.share|pct0}} sourced ({{stop_causes.likely.efficacy.share|pct0}} counting likely causes).
* **Sponsor decisions are the rest.** Portfolio, commercial and partner decisions, acquisitions, mergers, lost funding and closures account for {{stop_causes.likely.sponsor_decision.share|pct0}} of all stops counting likely causes. Safety stays rare ({{stop_causes.likely.safety.share|pct0}}).

**Within each cause.** Where the sponsor decided, the sources most often name portfolio prioritisation ({{stop_causes.subcauses.sponsor_decision.portfolio_prioritisation.share|pct0}}) or the company running out of money or closing ({{stop_causes.subcauses.sponsor_decision.funding_or_closure.share|pct0}}); {{stop_causes.subcauses.sponsor_decision.not_stated.share|pct0}} say no more than "business reasons". Operational stops are mostly slow enrollment ({{stop_causes.subcauses.operational.enrollment.share|pct0}}). {{stop_causes.subcauses.safety.not_stated.share|pct0}} of safety stops do not say what the harm was. Most positive readouts that went no further give no reason ({{stop_causes.subcauses.positive_not_advanced.not_stated.share|pct0}}); approval outside the US is the most common one named ({{stop_causes.subcauses.positive_not_advanced.approved_outside_us.share|pct0}}). Of the stops no source explains, {{stop_causes.subcauses.unknown.sponsor_went_quiet.share|pct0}} had a sponsor that started no later trial, and in {{stop_causes.subcauses.unknown.drug_dropped.share|pct0}} the drug was dropped while its sponsor carried on.

**Phase 1.** Phase 1 stops were classified the same way ({{stop_causes.phase1.stops|int}} programs entering 2008–2019). As registered, {{stop_causes.phase1.recorded.unknown.share|pct0}} give no usable reason, since Phase 1 trials seldom post results and companies seldom explain pausing early assets. Tracing found a sourced cause for {{stop_causes.phase1.inferred.resolved|pct0}} of those and a likely cause for {{stop_causes.phase1.inferred.likely_resolved|pct0}}. Counting both, efficacy or exposure was the cause of {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.efficacy.share|pct0}} of Phase 1 stops, safety {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.safety.share|pct0}}, sponsor decisions {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.sponsor_decision.share|pct0}}, operational {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.operational.share|pct0}} and a positive readout not yet advanced {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.positive_not_advanced.share|pct0}}; {{stop_causes.phase1.shown.unknown.share|pct0}} stay unknown.

Tested on stops whose reason is known, the classifier often read safety stops as efficacy, so the efficacy share after tracing is an upper estimate and safety is likely under-counted.
