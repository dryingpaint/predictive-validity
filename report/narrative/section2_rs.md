Rare-variant and Mendelian evidence ({{section2.rs.genetic_rare.rs|x2}}×) and GWAS evidence ({{section2.rs.genetic_gwas.rs|x2}}×) both carry the genetic signal, in line with Minikel et al. (2024), who found 2.6× from Phase 1 to launch with Mendelian evidence stronger than GWAS. Somatic driver evidence ({{section2.rs.somatic.rs|x2}}×), mouse phenotypes ({{section2.rs.animal_model.rs|x2}}×) and Reactome pathways ({{section2.rs.affected_pathway.rs|x2}}×, from only {{section2.rs.affected_pathway.n_with|int}} programs) also associate with success; differential expression in the disease does not clearly ({{section2.rs.rna_expression.rs|x2}}×, interval spanning 1). CRISPR knockout screens in cancer cell lines (Project Score) were first released in 2019, after every program in the window had entered its phase, so none could count them; scored on today's data instead, as hindsight, they give {{section2.rs_untimed.cell_line_screen_untimed.rs|x2}}× ({{section2.rs_untimed.cell_line_screen_untimed.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs_untimed.cell_line_screen_untimed.rs_hi|x2}}, {{section2.rs_untimed.cell_line_screen_untimed.n_with|int}} programs), mostly on targets oncology had already validated (EGFR, ERBB2, ESR1, KRAS, CDK4 and CDK6).

Target liabilities, properties of the target rather than evidence for the disease, carry little. Loss-of-function-intolerant targets show no penalty ({{section2.rs.constrained.rs|x2}}×). DepMap's common-essential list was also first published in 2019, so no program in the window could have known its target was on it. Judged by today's list, as hindsight, essential targets reach approval far less often ({{section2.rs_untimed.pan_essential_untimed.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs_untimed.pan_essential_untimed.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs_untimed.pan_essential_untimed.rs_hi|x2}}), but much of that comes from targets found essential after their programs had failed, mostly oncology small molecules aimed at machinery every cell needs (mTOR, tubulin, topoisomerases, histone deacetylases, WEE1 and ATR, BET proteins), so it is not a penalty a sponsor could have applied.

Two checks bear on whether this reflects foresight rather than hindsight. Evidence is taken as it stood the year before the program entered the phase, each item dated by when it became known, so later discoveries do not count. Counting all evidence known by 2026 instead weakens the genetic relative success to {{section2.rs_untimed.genetic_any_untimed.rs|x2}}×: what was known in advance is more telling than what was found afterwards. And restricting matches to diseases similar to the program's (Lin ≥ 0.8, Minikel's criterion, without the subtype rule) gives {{section2.rs_lin_only.genetic_any_strict.rs|x2}}×.
