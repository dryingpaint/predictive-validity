**Human genetics predicts at every step.** US-developed programs whose target had inherited variants linked to the disease before entry reached approval {{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.rs_rs|x2}} times as often ({{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.rs_rs_hi|x2}}; {{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.rs_loa_with|pct0}} against {{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.rs_loa_without|pct0}}), advanced from Phase 2 {{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.s4_rr|x2}} times as often, and stopped for efficacy less often ({{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.efficacy_stop_rr|x2}}×). Rare-variant and Mendelian evidence carries it ({{evidence_analysis.lines.rare_variant.rs_rs|x2}}× to approval, {{evidence_analysis.lines.rare_variant.s4_rr|x2}}× at Phase 2), as Minikel et al. (2024) found; GWAS evidence goes with approval ({{evidence_analysis.lines.gwas.rs_rs|x2}}×) but not with advancing from Phase 2 ({{evidence_analysis.lines.gwas.s4_rr|x2}}×). Across all programs, rare-variant evidence keeps an independent effect with every other line held fixed ({{section2.joint_rr.genetic_rare_2.rr|x2}}×). Curated disease mechanisms (Reactome) sit mostly on targets that already have genetic support and add nothing clear ({{evidence_analysis.lines.reactome.rs_rs|x2}}×, {{evidence_analysis.lines.reactome.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.reactome.rs_rs_hi|x2}}).

**Tumour genetics predicts in oncology; genetically altered mice predict less.** A gene recurrently mutated in the cancer went with approval {{evidence_analysis.lines.somatic.rs_rs|x2}} times as often ({{evidence_analysis.lines.somatic.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.somatic.rs_rs_hi|x2}}) and with advancing from Phase 2 ({{evidence_analysis.lines.somatic.s4_rr|x2}}×); requiring the mutation to match the drug adds nothing ({{evidence_analysis.lines.somatic_concordant.rs_rs|x2}}×). A genetically altered mouse model of the disease gives {{evidence_analysis.lines.altered_animal.rs_rs|x2}}× ({{evidence_analysis.lines.altered_animal.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.altered_animal.rs_rs_hi|x2}}), but across all programs it adds little once genetics is known ({{section2.joint_rr.animal_model_2.rr|x2}}×): such models mostly sit on genetically supported targets.

**Diseased tissue and the target's safety risk carry nothing.** A change in the target's RNA level in diseased tissue does not go with approval ({{evidence_analysis.lines.expression.rs_rs|x2}}×, {{evidence_analysis.lines.expression.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.expression.rs_rs_hi|x2}}). A target that does not tolerate loss of function, or whose knockout mouse dies, was no more likely to stop for safety ({{evidence_analysis.lines.liability.safety_stop_rr|x2}}×, {{evidence_analysis.lines.liability.safety_stop_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.liability.safety_stop_hi|x2}}).

**What earlier drugs on the target did predicts as strongly as genetics, in both directions.** An approved drug on the target for a related disease, the benchmark, goes with approval {{evidence_analysis.lines.b1.rs_rs|x2}} times as often and with advancing from Phase 2 {{evidence_analysis.lines.b1.s4_rr|x2}} times as often. An approved drug on the target only for unrelated diseases goes with less success ({{evidence_analysis.lines.approved_unrelated_only.rs_rs|x2}}× to approval, {{evidence_analysis.lines.approved_unrelated_only.s4_rr|x2}}× at Phase 2); the comparison includes the related-disease programs above, so a distant approval is at least no advantage. An earlier failed trial of another drug on the target in a related disease went with *more* success ({{evidence_analysis.lines.earlier_failure.rs_rs|x2}}×, {{evidence_analysis.lines.earlier_failure.s4_rr|x2}}× at Phase 2): such failures mark targets under heavy development, not targets known to be wrong.

Patient selection does not explain the results: holding biomarker-selected enrolment fixed leaves human genetics at {{evidence_analysis.lines.g3.s4_biomarker_rr|x2}}× at Phase 2.

**Known only after entry.** Genome-wide CRISPR screens beyond cancer cell lines (Project Score's priority targets, neuronal screens) and DepMap's essential-gene list were first published in 2019, too late for any program in the window. With hindsight, screen hits go with success ({{evidence_analysis.lines.crispr_untimed.rs_rs|x2}}× to approval), mostly on oncology targets already validated, and essential targets with failure ({{evidence_analysis.lines.pan_essential_untimed.rs_rs|x2}}×), much of it from targets found essential after their programs had failed. The genetics result itself is not hindsight: across all programs, counting evidence known by 2026 instead of before entry weakens it to {{section2.rs_untimed.genetic_any_untimed.rs|x2}}×.
