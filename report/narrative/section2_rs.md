Rare-variant and Mendelian evidence ({{section2.rs.genetic_rare.rs|x2}}×) and GWAS evidence ({{section2.rs.genetic_gwas.rs|x2}}×) both carry the genetic signal, in line with Minikel et al. (2024), who found 2.6× from Phase 1 to launch with Mendelian evidence stronger than GWAS. Somatic driver evidence ({{section2.rs.somatic.rs|x2}}×), mouse phenotypes ({{section2.rs.animal_model.rs|x2}}×) and Reactome pathways ({{section2.rs.affected_pathway.rs|x2}}×, from only {{section2.rs.affected_pathway.n_with|int}} programs) also associate with success; differential expression in the disease does not clearly ({{section2.rs.rna_expression.rs|x2}}×, interval spanning 1). CRISPR knockout screens in cancer cell lines show the largest ratio ({{section2.rs.cell_line_screen.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs.cell_line_screen.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.cell_line_screen.rs_hi|x2}}, {{section2.rs.cell_line_screen.n_with|int}} programs), but they mostly flag targets oncology had already validated (EGFR, ERBB2, ESR1, KRAS, CDK4 and CDK6), {{section2.cell_line_screen_somatic|pct0}} of them also somatic drivers for the cancer; they add nothing once the other lines are in the model (below). Only Project Score pairs listed since its 2019 release count before 2024. The neuron screens matched too few programs ({{section2.rs.neuron_screen.n_with|int}}) to estimate.

Target liabilities, properties of the target rather than evidence for the disease, carry little. Loss-of-function-intolerant targets show no penalty ({{section2.rs.constrained.rs|x2}}×). Targets on DepMap's common-essential list before entry reach approval less often ({{section2.rs.pan_essential.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs.pan_essential.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.pan_essential.rs_hi|x2}}), but the interval is wide: the list was first published in 2019, so only the {{section2.rs.pan_essential.n_with|int}} programs that entered a phase from 2020 can carry the flag. Judged by today's list instead, as earlier drafts did, the penalty looks firm ({{section2.rs_untimed.pan_essential_untimed.rs|x2}}×, {{section2.rs_untimed.pan_essential_untimed.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs_untimed.pan_essential_untimed.rs_hi|x2}}): much of it comes from targets found essential after their programs had started, mostly oncology small molecules aimed at machinery every cell needs (mTOR, tubulin, topoisomerases, histone deacetylases, WEE1 and ATR, BET proteins).

Two checks bear on whether this reflects foresight rather than hindsight. Evidence is taken as it stood the year before the program entered the phase, each item dated by when it became known, so later discoveries do not count. Counting all evidence known by 2026 instead weakens the genetic relative success to {{section2.rs_untimed.genetic_any_untimed.rs|x2}}×: what was known in advance is more telling than what was found afterwards.
