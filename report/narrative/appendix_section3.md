### Design of the Phase 2 analysis

* **Cohort and outcome.** Programs on a human protein target that entered Phase 2 from {{section3.window.start}} to {{section3.window.end}} ({{section3.c2.programs|int}} programs on {{section3.c2.targets|int}} primary-target sets). Each has at least four years of follow-up before the 24-month inactivity cutoff, so the outcome needs no inferred suspension: a program *advanced* if a Phase 3 trial of its own started, or the FDA approved it for the disease, within four years of entering Phase 2 ({{section3.c2.s4|pct}} did). The same is done for Phase 1 entrants (a negative control: genetics should matter little for safety and dosing studies) and Phase 3 entrants (approval within four years).
* **Evidence.** Every Open Targets 26.09 evidence row for the program's primary targets, each dated by the year it became known (ClinVar records by their creation, IMPC knockouts by their first data release, otherwise the row's own publication), frozen at the year before entry and matched to the program's disease on MONDO: the same term, a subtype, or Lin similarity ≥ 0.8 (*genetics for the disease*); a disease at Lin 0.5–0.8 only (*genetics for a related disease*); germline evidence only for unrelated diseases; or none. Evidence is split by family and strength (the best GWAS locus-to-gene score, ≥ 0.8, 0.5–0.8 or below; ClinVar pathogenic with multiple submitters or expert review, ClinGen or Gene2Phenotype definitive or strong, PanelApp green; mouse phenotype match in the top quartile), and mouse models are split into IMPC high-throughput knockouts and literature-curated MGI models.
* **Regimes.** Oncology; *hereditary* (the disease is a MONDO hereditary disease and the target has curated Mendelian evidence for it); and every other disease (*non-oncology*, the regime of the headline hypothesis).
* **Models.** Modified Poisson regression (risk ratios) with standard errors clustered by primary-target set, adjusted for disease area, modality, entity novelty, entry period and target precedent (an FDA-approved entity on the target before entry). The seven confirmatory hypotheses are Holm-corrected together.
* **Trials.** Randomised industry Phase 2/3 trials started in 2008–2019 whose governing primary endpoint measures efficacy and whose result is readable ({{section3.trials.n|int}} trials; an LLM classified each trial's governing primary endpoint from its registry entry, without the drug's name). Outcome: primary endpoint not met (a mixed result counts as not met).

**Registered rule screen.** The plan tested fifteen rules on their own, each against all other programs, with a split of target sets in place of a correction for multiple testing. Because the rules overlap and the split halves the data, the body of the report groups them by kind of evidence instead (a change from the plan). The registered screen, as run: target sets were split 60/40 at random; a rule counts as *particularly predictive* when, on the discovery targets, its risk ratio's 90% bootstrap lower bound is above 1.3, and, on the held-out targets, its risk ratio is at least 1.2 with a one-sided 95% lower bound above 1, and it stays at least 1.2 without its most influential target.

**Case studies.** An earlier version of this analysis, on 2015–2020 Phase 2 entries, checked 75 cases on 62 programs picked for the best-performing rules against registry records, filings, papers and FDA documents, one agent establishing the outcome without seeing the evidence and another checking the evidence without seeing the outcome. The recorded outcome was right for 58 of 62 programs (each error a program recorded as stopped that had advanced, through a Phase 3 registered under another condition, run only in China, or not linked to the program); the evidence claim held for 49 of 75 cases and partly for 19; and of the 30 programs that did not advance, 16 failed for reasons that test the evidence's biology. The report's case studies are now the historical cases in the main text.

**Checks on the genetics result.** Swapping each program's disease for another program's in the same disease area, {{section3.permutation.n_permutations|int}} times, gives a median risk ratio for genetics for the disease of {{section3.permutation.null_median|x2}} against the observed {{section3.permutation.observed|x2}} (95th percentile {{section3.permutation.null_95|x2}}; p = {{section3.permutation.p_one_sided|x3}}). Much of the contrast with targets that have no germline genetics therefore reflects whether a target has been studied genetically at all; matching the disease adds to it, though the observed value sits at the edge of what shuffled diseases give. At Phase 1, where efficacy is rarely tested, the same contrast is {{section3.controls.phase1_g3.rr|x2}} ({{section3.controls.phase1_g3.lo|x2}}–{{section3.controls.phase1_g3.hi|x2}}). Against all other programs rather than those with no germline genetics on the target, genetic support gives {{section3.map.C2_COM.g3.rr|x2}} outside oncology, and {{section3.beyond_precedent.COM.adjusted_for_b1.rr|x2}} ({{section3.beyond_precedent.COM.adjusted_for_b1.lo|x2}}–{{section3.beyond_precedent.COM.adjusted_for_b1.hi|x2}}) after adjusting for an approved drug on the target for a related disease; on targets with no approved drug at all, genetic support gives {{section3.beyond_precedent.C2.novel_targets.rr|x2}} ({{section3.beyond_precedent.C2.novel_targets.lo|x2}}–{{section3.beyond_precedent.C2.novel_targets.hi|x2}}, all disease areas). Leaving out the most influential target set ({{section3.influence.most_influential}}) gives {{section3.influence.rr_without|x2}}. Leaving out each of the {{section3.influence.classes|int}} largest target classes in turn gives {{section3.influence.class_min.rr|x2}} to {{section3.influence.class_max|x2}}; the lowest, without the "{{section3.influence.lowest_class}}" class, has an interval of {{section3.influence.class_min.lo|x2}}–{{section3.influence.class_min.hi|x2}}, so the estimate leans on that class.

**Who is in the cohort.** The cohort is Phase 2 programs with a disease and a single resolved human protein target. Left out are {{section3.excluded.no_disease.programs|int}} programs with no disease (healthy-volunteer, basket or symptom trials; {{section3.excluded.no_disease.s4|pct0}} advanced) and {{section3.excluded.no_protein_target.programs|int}} without a single human protein target ({{section3.excluded.no_protein_target.s4|pct0}}), so the cohort's base rate is higher than Section 1's.

**Each evidence family.**

* **Human genetics.** Mendelian and ClinVar evidence for the disease ({{section3.map.C2.mendelian.rr|x2}}; top-tier curation {{section3.map.C2.mendelian_strong.rr|x2}}) and GWAS ({{section3.map.C2.gwas.rr|x2}}) both carry the signal. Within GWAS, the locus-to-gene score did not grade it: the best score at 0.8 or above gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_strong.rr|x2}}, 0.5–0.8 gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_mid.rr|x2}} and below 0.5 gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_weak.rr|x2}}, each from a few dozen programs. Most GWAS rows in Open Targets are on measurement traits (LDL cholesterol, blood pressure, body-mass index) with no MONDO disease; they count as germline evidence on the target but cannot match a disease, so genetics for the disease is undercounted when it was found through a trait. Gene burden results start in 2019, too late for these cohorts.
* **Somatic drivers.** In oncology, a somatic driver for the tumour raised the chance of advancing ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.rr|x2}}, {{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.hi|x2}}); requiring the driver's direction to match the drug's did not sharpen it ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic_concordant.rr|x2}}). Germline genetics for the tumour type was the stronger signal ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.g3.rr|x2}}), carried by well-validated targets such as EGFR, ERBB2 and ESR1.
* **Mouse models.** Literature-curated (MGI) mouse models of the disease gave {{section3.map.C2.mgi.rr|x2}} ({{section3.map.C2.mgi.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2.mgi.hi|x2}}), and a top-quartile phenotype match {{section3.map.C2.mgi_strong.rr|x2}}. Each MGI model is dated by its first paper, and each knockout from the IMPC consortia by the first IMPC data release with calls for it (2015 at the earliest).
* **Expression and pathways.** Differential expression in the disease carried nothing ({{section3.map.C2.expression.rr|x2}}; outside oncology {{section3.map.C2_COM.expression.rr|x2}}). Reactome pathway evidence gave {{section3.map.C2.reactome.rr|x2}} from {{section3.map.C2.reactome.n|int}} programs, most of them in hereditary diseases, which Reactome curates.
* **Clinical precedent.** An FDA-approved drug on the target for a related disease (Lin similarity ≥ 0.5) was the strongest single predictor ({{section3.map.C2.b1.rr|x2}}) and was present for {{section3.coverage.with_b1|pct0}} of the programs that advanced.
* **Target liabilities.** Loss-of-function intolerance ({{section3.map.C2.constrained.rr|x2}}) and lethality of the mouse knockout ({{section3.map.C2.ko_lethal.rr|x2}}) made no difference. Essentiality across cell lines could not be known before entry for any program in the window (above).

**What the evidence leaves out.** Of the {{section3.coverage.successes|int}} Phase 2 programs that advanced, {{section3.coverage.with_g3|pct0}} had germline genetics for the disease, {{section3.coverage.with_any_evidence|pct0}} had any of the disease-specific evidence families, and {{section3.coverage.neither|pct0}} had neither such evidence nor an approved drug on the target for a related disease.

**Direction of effect.** A drug that lowers its target's function should help when loss of function protects against the disease or gain of function causes it, and harm or do nothing when loss of function causes it. Open Targets' direction fields cannot test this: they label each variant from its predicted consequence, so the few truncating ClinVar variants listed for PCSK9 in hypercholesterolaemia, MYH7 in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy or TTR in amyloidosis make these gain-of-function diseases read as loss of function; their many pathogenic missense variants carry no direction at all. We therefore had an LLM state, for every target and disease with germline evidence, without being told any drug, whether disease-causing variants lower or raise the gene's function, whether protective variants are known, and the year each was first shown for that disease. Where Gene2Phenotype records an absent gene product, the label was loss of function in {{section3.mechanism.g2p.absent_product_lof|int}} of {{section3.mechanism.g2p.absent_product|int}} pairs and gain of function in {{section3.mechanism.g2p.absent_product_gof|int}}. A spot check of an earlier version of the labels found somatic cancer mutations counted as germline and some years taken from another disease; the final prompt rules out both. Sponsors almost never develop against an established mechanism. Of the Phase 2 programs with genetic support, {{section3.mechanism.levels.concordant.programs|int}} acted in the direction the genetics favoured, {{section3.mechanism.levels.discordant.programs|int}} against it, and {{section3.mechanism.levels.undirected.programs|int}} had genetics without an established direction by entry. Concordant programs advanced at {{section3.mechanism.levels.concordant.s4|pct0}} and undirected ones at {{section3.mechanism.levels.undirected.s4|pct0}}: once there is genetic support, knowing its direction did not pick winners among the programs sponsors chose to run, and the discordant group is too small to show whether going against the genetics fails. Protective loss of function plus a drug that lowers function, the PCSK9 pattern, covered {{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.n|int}} Phase 2 programs on {{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.n_targets|int}} targets, which advanced as often as others ({{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.rr|x2}}, {{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.lo|x2}}–{{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.hi|x2}}). Mouse models were treated the same way: for each model, without the drug, whether it lowers or raises the gene's function and whether its phenotypes resemble the disease or its opposite. A model pointing the drug's way (a knockout protected from the disease, for an inhibitor) gave {{section3.mouse.vs_none.concordant.rr|x2}} ({{section3.mouse.vs_none.concordant.lo|x2}}–{{section3.mouse.vs_none.concordant.hi|x2}}) against programs with no oriented model, a model pointing the other way {{section3.mouse.vs_none.discordant.rr|x2}}, and nothing once genetic support is accounted for ({{section3.mouse.concordant_beyond_g3.rr|x2}}). Among Phase 3 programs a concordant model went with approval within four years ({{section3.mouse.c3.concordant.rr|x2}}, {{section3.mouse.c3.concordant.lo|x2}}–{{section3.mouse.c3.concordant.hi|x2}}), one of many comparisons made after the plan.

**Endpoints at the program level.** At the program level, classifying each Phase 2 program by the endpoint of its first randomised Phase 2 efficacy trial (started within a year of entry and before it advanced), genetic support gives {{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.proximal.rr|x2}} ({{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.proximal.lo|x2}}–{{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.proximal.hi|x2}}; {{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.proximal.programs|int}} programs) where that endpoint was proximal and {{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.intermediate.rr|x2}} ({{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.intermediate.lo|x2}}–{{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.intermediate.hi|x2}}; {{section3.by_endpoint_stratum.intermediate.programs|int}}) where it was intermediate; distal-endpoint and oncology programs had too few genetically supported targets to estimate. The endpoint classifier read each trial's registered primary outcomes without the drug. In an audit of {{section3.endpoint_audit.trials|int}} trials stratified by class, it picked the governing outcome correctly in {{section3.endpoint_audit.governing_index_correct|int}} and the class in {{section3.endpoint_audit.class_correct|int}}. Not knowing the drug, it cannot apply the codebook's drug-specific rules: FEV1 is a regulatory surrogate only for bronchodilators, and target engagement means the drug's own target. Trials still in follow-up that have posted results have no outcome label yet and are not counted ({{section3.trials.unlabelled_with_results|int}}, against {{section3.trials.n|int}} in the analysis).
