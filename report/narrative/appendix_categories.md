### Evidence categories

**Cohort.** Phase entries in 2008–2019 with an industry trial sited in the US in that phase: {{evidence_analysis.cohort.entries|int}} entries of {{evidence_analysis.cohort.programs|int}} programs, {{evidence_analysis.cohort.with_target_disease|int}} of them with a single resolved human protein target and a disease. Every line is frozen at the year before entry.

**Categories.** The plan was amended on 2026-11-03, before any line below was compared with an outcome, to group efficacy evidence by the experimental system that produced it:

* **Human genetics:** germline evidence for the disease, split into rare-variant or Mendelian and GWAS, both together, genetics pointing the same way as the drug, and curated disease mechanisms (Reactome).
* **Tumour genetics** (oncology only): a gene recurrently mutated in the cancer, and a mutation that matches the drug.
* **Human diseased tissue:** the target's RNA level changed by at least 2.1-fold in diseased tissue against normal tissue, and the same with a drug that lowers the target's function.
* **Animal studies:** mouse genetics, mice with the target gene knocked out or mutated whose phenotype resembles the disease (literature-curated and systematic knockout screens, a close phenotype match, a model pointing the same way as the drug). Studies of the drug itself in animals are described below.
* **Cell studies** (oncology entries from 2015): the target is a dependency in cancer cell lines of the program's tumour type (gene effect −0.5 or lower in at least two lines and 20% of the type's screened lines, genes on that year's common-essential list excluded), in genome-wide RNAi screens (Achilles, Project DRIVE, Marcotte breast lines; DEMETER2 scores) and CRISPR screens (Avana; CERES scores), each line dated by the paper or release that first made it public (2014–2018). The scores are 2018–19 reprocessings of those screens: the screen data existed before entry, but these exact scores were computed later. Project Score's CRISPR priority targets, first published in 2019, are scored only on today's data, as hindsight.
* **The molecule** (small molecules): best potency at the primary target of 100 nM or better, from assays published before entry (the plan first read 10 nM, a typo corrected on 2026-11-04 before any outcome was tested); at least 10-fold selectivity over the best off-target, among drugs measured on both; drug-like properties (at most one Rule-of-5 violation); and, for neurology and psychiatry programs, brain-penetrant properties (polar surface area under 90 Å², molecular weight under 450, at most three hydrogen-bond donors). Properties are PubChem's computed values, or ChEMBL's for molecules PubChem does not list; a structure does not change, so they hold at any entry. Biologics have no potency line: ChEMBL holds no potency measurement at a human target for any antibody, bispecific antibody or antibody–drug conjugate with a Phase 2 or later program, and a probe of 40 antibody and protein programs found that the affinities published before entry are almost all 100 nM or better (16 of 17), so a line at that threshold would mark only whether a value had been published.
* **Early human pharmacology:** target engagement measured in people before entry (described below).
* **Safety risk**, kept apart: the target does not tolerate loss of function, its knockout mouse dies before weaning, or it is essential in most cell lines (hindsight only).
* **Clinical precedent**, the benchmark: an approved drug on the target for a related disease (Lin similarity ≥ 0.5); for unrelated diseases only; and an earlier Phase 2 or 3 trial of another drug on the target in a related disease that missed its primary endpoint or stopped for futility within the three years before entry. The last is scored for 2011–2019 entries only, because trial results are sparse before 2008 and a lookback reaching before them would make the line rise with entry year.

**The chart's areas.** The evidence chart regroups these categories into areas that each answer one question, with groups of programs that do not overlap (for human genetics: rare variants only, common variants only, both). The grouping was chosen on 2026-11-05, after the category results were seen, so it is exploratory; the categories in the table below remain the analysis the plan registered. Cancer-cell dependency is left off the chart: it can be scored only on cancer programs entering from 2015, when the cell-line screens began, and its range is too wide to say whether it helps or hurts: a target the tumour type's cell lines depend on went with approval {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.rs_rs|x2}}× ({{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.rs_rs_hi|x2}}; {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.exposed|int}} of {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.eligible|int}} entries) and with advancing from Phase 2 {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.s4_rr|x2}}×.

**Lines tried but not measured.** The chart draws only lines with an estimate. The table below also lists the ones we tried and could not measure: a line held by too few programs to estimate (both common and rare variants for the same disease, and the large-scale mouse knockout screen); a line whose data first appeared in 2019, after every program in the window had started (CRISPR screens and the list of genes most cell types need, which the table also shows with hindsight); and the three lines read from the published literature (the drug in an animal model of the disease, the drug tested in healthy animals, and the drug shown to act on its target in people), whose searches found too few of the studies that exist to be measured.

**How a category is formed.** An entry has a category when it has any of the category's subcategories, and lacks it when it has none of them. Only subcategories scored on all of the category's entries count, so brain-penetrant properties and the earlier failed trial are reported on their own: the clinical-precedent category is an approved drug on the target for any disease. The molecule category covers small molecules whose computed properties are known. An entry on which none of a category's subcategories is known has no value. The first build treated unknown as absent: for the molecule category, 582 of the small molecules counted as lacking it had no known structure and no measurement. That build was corrected on 2026-11-04, before the category results were published.

**Estimates.** For each line, among the entries it applies to: relative success to approval (skipped phases count as passed and carry the features of the entry that skipped them; intervals resample primary-target sets); the risk ratio of advancing within four years of Phase 2 entry, from the modified Poisson model of the Phase 2 analysis (adjusted for disease area, modality, entry period, novelty and target precedent, errors clustered by primary-target set; an entry with no resolved target is its own cluster); and, for efficacy categories, the risk ratio of an efficacy stop among Phase 2 and 3 entries, or for safety risk of a safety stop among Phase 1–3 entries. A line with fewer than {{evidence_analysis.cohort.min_entries|int}} exposed entries or {{evidence_analysis.cohort.min_targets|int}} distinct target sets is described, not estimated. As a sensitivity, each Phase 2 model also holds fixed whether the trials that opened the phase enrolled patients by a molecular biomarker, which an LLM read from their eligibility criteria ({{evidence_analysis.cohort.biomarker_selected|pct0}} of entries). Lines already reported before the amendment (human genetics, tumour genetics, diseased tissue, mouse genetics, safety risk, potency) were regrouped knowing their results, so their category estimates are not new confirmations.

**Hypotheses added.** N1 (selectivity), N2 (drug-likeness, and brain-penetrant properties in neurology and psychiatry, tested together by taking the larger p) and N3 (potency outside oncology, with human genetics and drug-likeness held fixed) are Holm-corrected as a family of three. N3 first tested the whole molecule category; on 2026-11-04, before any outcome was tested, it was narrowed to potency, because drug-likeness alone covered most small molecules and would have dominated the category.

**Literature lines.** Whether the drug itself had been tested in an animal model of the disease, its pharmacology in healthy animals, and target engagement measured in people were to come from published abstracts, screened by an LLM. The plan admitted them only if, on a seeded pilot, retrieval found at least {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.gates.recall|pct0}} of the qualifying studies that agents with web access listed blind, a second model agreed with the screen (κ ≥ {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.gates.kappa|x2}}), and at least {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.gates.phase2_coverage|pct0}} of Phase 2 entries carried the line. Screening was accurate, but retrieval was not: a search by drug and disease found {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.animal_disease.recall|pct0}}, {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.animal_healthy.recall|pct0}} and {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.human_engagement.recall|pct0}} of the reference studies of the three kinds. One second version, fixed in advance and scored on a fresh reference set, searched by every name of the drug alone and found {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v2.animal_disease.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}}, {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v2.animal_healthy.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}} and {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v2.human_engagement.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}} of the reference studies indexed in PubMed. Neither version passed, so the three lines are left out of every estimate. The plan stopped there; at the user's direction it was amended to allow a third attempt, with no cap on abstracts per drug and with Firecrawl's research index, scored on a third blind reference set. Any line that passes will be reported as passing on a third, user-directed attempt. Papers name animal models by their assay rather than the disease, and well-studied drugs have more abstracts than any search can keep; much of this evidence also sits in conference abstracts and company filings that no search of abstracts reaches.
