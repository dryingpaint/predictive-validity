### Kinds of evidence

**Cohort.** Phase entries in 2008–2019 with an industry trial sited in the US in that phase: {{evidence_analysis.cohort.entries|int}} entries of {{evidence_analysis.cohort.programs|int}} programs, {{evidence_analysis.cohort.with_target_disease|int}} of them with a single resolved human protein target and a disease. Every line is frozen at the year before entry.

**Structure.** Evidence is grouped by the experimental system that produced it, in the broad categories of one list of kinds of evidence shared by the evidence chart and the label of where each target hypothesis came from (agreed 2026-11-20). Each kind is evidence about the target or about the drug, and is made of facts: questions answered by exactly one value for every entry they apply to, such as which inherited variants link the gene to the disease (rare only, common only, both, none). Whether evidence agrees with the drug's direction is not a fact of its own: it crosses a fact about the gene with what the drug does to its target. No kind is split by the database it comes from. A kind that exists only for some diseases is compared only within them: entries outside its scope have no value, not a "no".

* **Human genetics.**
  * **Human genetics:** germline evidence for the disease (rare-variant or Mendelian, GWAS, or both), and what losing the gene does to disease risk by the labelled mechanism.
  * **Tumour genetics** (oncology): the gene recurrently mutated in the cancer, by whether the mutations activate or inactivate it.
  * **Curated pathway:** a curated human disease mechanism (Reactome's human pathways) shows a mutation that causes the disease in people disrupting the target.
* **In vitro and ex vivo:** human cells and tissue studied outside the body, whether measured as taken from patients or changed by experiment.
  * **Diseased tissue:** the target's RNA level changed by at least 2.1-fold in diseased tissue against normal tissue, higher in at least one comparison or lower only. Protein in patients' tissue is on the list but not yet built.
  * Changing the target in primary human cells or tissue is on the list but not yet built.
  * **Changing the target in an established cell line changes a disease feature** (today oncology, entries from 2015, until the literature part is built): every source counts on the entries it reaches, and an entry no source reaches has no value. Today the only source is knockout screens: in screens published before entry, cancer cell lines of the program's tumour type need the gene (gene effect −0.5 or lower in at least two lines and 20% of the type's screened lines, genes on that year's common-essential list excluded). The screens are genome-wide RNAi screens (Achilles, Project DRIVE, Marcotte breast lines; DEMETER2 scores) and CRISPR screens (Avana; CERES scores), each line dated by the paper or release that first made it public (2014–2018), and Project Score's CRISPR priority targets, first published in 2019, which reach no entry in the window and are also shown on today's data, as hindsight. The scores are 2018–19 reprocessings of those screens: the screen data existed before entry, but these exact scores were computed later.
* **Animals.**
  * **Mouse genetics:** mice with the target gene knocked out or mutated whose phenotype resembles the disease, by how closely, and which way the models favour moving the gene's function.
  * **Animal model pharmacology:** a non-genetic change to the target (a drug, antibody or siRNA) alters an animal model of the disease. The agent does not split it: today its only source is published studies of the program's own drug in a model of the disease, and studies of other agents will pool into it once they are read from the literature (described below).
* **Human.**
  * An observational biomarker and clinical observations are on the list but not yet built; they will be read from the literature.
  * **Approved on the target:** a drug on the target was already approved before entry, in one of three bands of how close its disease is to the program's: the same or a very close disease (Lin similarity ≥ 0.8; the pair is *validated at entry*), a related disease (Lin 0.5 to 0.8), or only unrelated diseases. The bands are one kind because they are one fact, but they point in opposite directions, so the chart and the stop analysis show them apart.
  * **Another drug on the target failed in a related disease:** a Phase 2 or 3 trial of another drug on the target in a related disease (Lin ≥ 0.5) missed its primary endpoint or stopped for futility within the three years before entry. It is scored for 2011–2019 entries only, because trial results are sparse before 2008 and a lookback reaching before them would make the line rise with entry year.
* **The drug** (about the drug; none of it says the target is right).
  * **Binding** (small molecules): best potency at the primary target from assays published before entry, 10 nM or better, 10 to 100 nM, or weaker (the plan first read 10 nM for its line, a typo corrected on 2026-11-04 before any outcome was tested), selectivity over the best off-target, among drugs measured on both, and whether the drug's effect was measured in living cells (a functional, cell-based assay at a primary target published before entry), which the compiled dataset gained on 2026-11-20 after Section 2 had already reported it. Biologics have no potency fact: ChEMBL holds no potency measurement at a human target for any antibody, bispecific antibody or antibody–drug conjugate with a Phase 2 or later program, and a probe of 40 antibody and protein programs found that the affinities published before entry are almost all 100 nM or better (16 of 17), so a fact at that threshold would mark only whether a value had been published. Safety-pharmacology assays such as hERG and cytochrome P450 inhibition are not included: few are published before entry, and those that are are not a fair sample.
  * **Pharmacokinetics or target engagement measured in healthy animals** (described below).
  * **The drug shown to act on its target in people** (described below).
  * **Molecule properties** (small molecules; stored and in the table below, not drawn on the chart): drug-like properties (at most one Rule-of-5 violation) and, for neurology and psychiatry programs, brain-penetrant properties (polar surface area under 90 Å², molecular weight under 450, at most three hydrogen-bond donors). Properties are PubChem's computed values, or ChEMBL's for molecules PubChem does not list; a structure does not change, so they hold at any entry.
* **Target safety risk**, kept apart: the target does not tolerate loss of function, its knockout mouse dies before weaning, or it is essential in most cell lines (no list before 2019, so shown with hindsight). Two exploratory safety facts, added on 2026-11-08, are tested against safety stops but make up no kind: a mutant-mouse phenotype in a vital organ, and an earlier drug on the target stopped for safety.

**The chart.** The evidence chart draws each kind with an estimate under a heading for its broad category, with marks coloured by whether the kind is about the target or the drug, and under some kinds the values of one of their facts, which do not overlap (for human genetics: rare variants only, common variants only, both). The cell-line row rests on cancer programs entering from 2015, when the cell-line screens began, and its range is wide: a target the tumour type's cell lines depend on in the dependency screens went with approval {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.rs_rs|x2}}× ({{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.rs_rs_lo|x2}}–{{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.rs_rs_hi|x2}}; {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.exposed|int}} of {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.eligible|int}} entries) and with advancing from Phase 2 {{evidence_analysis.lines.cell_dependency.s4_rr|x2}}×.

**Lines tried but not measured.** The chart draws only lines with an estimate. The table below also lists the ones we tried and could not measure: a line held by too few programs to estimate (such as both common and rare variants for the same disease, and the large-scale mouse knockout screen); and a line whose data first appeared in 2019, after every program in the window had started (CRISPR screens and the list of genes most cell types need, which the table also shows with hindsight). The lines read from the published literature are drawn but marked exploratory, because their searches missed part of the studies that exist (below).

**How a kind is formed.** An entry has a kind of evidence when any fact that makes it up takes one of the kind's evidence values, and lacks it when none does. Each fact counts on the entries it applies to, so a kind's absence rests on whichever facts reach that entry: cancer-cell dependency counts only for cancer programs from 2015, brain-penetrant properties only for brain diseases. Hindsight and exploratory facts do not count. An entry on which every fact of a kind is unknown (not measured, not searched, not scored) has no value. On 2026-11-20 these kinds and broad categories replaced the categories the plan registered on 2026-11-03: the registered categories did not say which evidence was about the target and which about the drug, and the chart and the origin label each used their own grouping. The lines that split a kind by source (literature-curated and screened mice, the dependency screens and Project Score) are read from the source data and estimated in the table below, not stored as facts. Every line the categories held is still estimated, in the table below, from the same facts; the kinds and their values are new groupings chosen after the category results were seen, so they are exploratory. The first build treated unknown as absent: for the molecule category, 582 of the small molecules counted as lacking it had no known structure and no measurement. That build was corrected on 2026-11-04, before the category results were published.

**Estimates.** For each line, among the entries it applies to: relative success to approval (skipped phases count as passed and carry the features of the entry that skipped them; intervals resample primary-target sets); the risk ratio of advancing within four years of Phase 2 entry, from the modified Poisson model of the Phase 2 analysis (adjusted for disease area, modality, entry period, novelty and target precedent, errors clustered by primary-target set; an entry with no resolved target is its own cluster); and, for evidence about the target or the drug, the risk ratio of an efficacy stop among Phase 2 and 3 entries, or for target safety risk of a safety stop among Phase 1–3 entries. A line with fewer than {{evidence_analysis.cohort.min_entries|int}} exposed entries or {{evidence_analysis.cohort.min_targets|int}} distinct target sets is described, not estimated. As a sensitivity, each Phase 2 model also holds fixed whether the trials that opened the phase enrolled patients by a molecular biomarker, which an LLM read from their eligibility criteria ({{evidence_analysis.cohort.biomarker_selected|pct0}} of entries). Lines already reported before the amendment (human genetics, tumour genetics, diseased tissue, mouse genetics, safety risk, potency) were regrouped knowing their results, so their estimates by kind are not new confirmations.

**Hypotheses added.** N1 (selectivity), N2 (drug-likeness, and brain-penetrant properties in neurology and psychiatry, tested together by taking the larger p) and N3 (potency outside oncology, with human genetics and drug-likeness held fixed) are Holm-corrected as a family of three. N3 first tested the whole molecule category; on 2026-11-04, before any outcome was tested, it was narrowed to potency, because drug-likeness alone covered most small molecules and would have dominated the category.

**Literature lines.** Whether the drug itself had been tested in an animal model of the disease, its pharmacology in healthy animals, and target engagement measured in people were to come from published abstracts, screened by an LLM. The plan admitted them only if, on a seeded pilot, retrieval found at least {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.gates.recall|pct0}} of the qualifying studies that agents with web access listed blind, a second model agreed with the screen (κ ≥ {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.gates.kappa|x2}}), and at least {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.gates.phase2_coverage|pct0}} of Phase 2 entries carried the line. Screening was accurate, but retrieval was not: a search by drug and disease found {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.animal_disease.recall|pct0}}, {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.animal_healthy.recall|pct0}} and {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v1.human_engagement.recall|pct0}} of the reference studies of the three kinds. One second version, fixed in advance and scored on a fresh reference set, searched by every name of the drug alone and found {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v2.animal_disease.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}}, {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v2.animal_healthy.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}} and {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v2.human_engagement.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}} of the reference studies indexed in PubMed. Neither version passed. At the user's direction the plan was amended to allow a third attempt, with no cap on abstracts per drug and with Firecrawl's research index, scored on a third blind reference set. It found {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v3_final.animal_disease.recall|pct0}} of the reference studies of the drug in an animal model of the disease ({{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v3_final.animal_disease.recall_pubmed_indexed|pct0}} of those indexed in PubMed), {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v3_final.animal_healthy.recall|pct0}} of those in healthy animals and {{evidence_analysis.literature_gate.v3_final.human_engagement.recall|pct0}} of those measuring target engagement in people, so none passes as registered. At the user's direction the three lines are reported as exploratory: the chart draws them, marked, and no conclusion of the report rests on them. Studies missed at random would pull each estimate toward no difference; they would not reverse it. Papers name animal models by their assay rather than the disease, and well-studied drugs have more abstracts than any search can keep; much of this evidence also sits in conference abstracts and company filings that no search of abstracts reaches.
