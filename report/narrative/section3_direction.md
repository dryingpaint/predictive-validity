A drug that lowers its target's function should help when loss of function protects against the disease or gain of function causes it, and harm or do nothing when loss of function causes it. Open Targets' direction fields cannot test this: they label each variant from its predicted consequence, so the few truncating ClinVar variants listed for PCSK9 in hypercholesterolaemia, MYH7 in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy or TTR in amyloidosis make these gain-of-function diseases read as loss of function; their many pathogenic missense variants carry no direction at all.

We therefore had an LLM state, for every target and disease with germline evidence, without being told any drug, whether disease-causing variants lower or raise the gene's function, whether protective variants are known, and the year each was first shown for that disease. Where Gene2Phenotype records an absent gene product, the label was loss of function in {{section3.mechanism.g2p.absent_product_lof|int}} of {{section3.mechanism.g2p.absent_product|int}} pairs and never gain of function. A spot check of an earlier version of the labels found somatic cancer mutations counted as germline and some years taken from another disease; the final prompt rules out both.

Sponsors almost never develop against an established mechanism. Of the Phase 2 programs with genetic support, {{section3.mechanism.levels.concordant.programs|int}} acted in the direction the genetics favoured, {{section3.mechanism.levels.discordant.programs|int}} against it, and {{section3.mechanism.levels.undirected.programs|int}} had genetics without an established direction by entry. Concordant programs advanced at {{section3.mechanism.levels.concordant.s4|pct0}} and undirected ones at {{section3.mechanism.levels.undirected.s4|pct0}}: once there is genetic support, knowing its direction did not pick winners among the programs sponsors chose to run, and the discordant group is too small to show whether going against the genetics fails. Protective loss of function plus a drug that lowers function, the PCSK9 pattern, covered {{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.n|int}} Phase 2 programs on {{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.n_targets|int}} targets, which advanced as often as others ({{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.rr|x2}}, {{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.lo|x2}}–{{section3.mechanism.lof_protective_decrease.hi|x2}}).

Mouse models were treated the same way: for each model, without the drug, whether it lowers or raises the gene's function and whether its phenotypes resemble the disease or its opposite. A model pointing the drug's way (a knockout protected from the disease, for an inhibitor) gave {{section3.mouse.vs_none.concordant.rr|x2}} ({{section3.mouse.vs_none.concordant.lo|x2}}–{{section3.mouse.vs_none.concordant.hi|x2}}) against programs with no oriented model, a model pointing the other way {{section3.mouse.vs_none.discordant.rr|x2}}, and nothing once genetic support is accounted for ({{section3.mouse.concordant_beyond_g3.rr|x2}}). Among Phase 3 programs a concordant model went with approval within four years ({{section3.mouse.c3.concordant.rr|x2}}, {{section3.mouse.c3.concordant.lo|x2}}–{{section3.mouse.c3.concordant.hi|x2}}), one of many comparisons made after the plan.
