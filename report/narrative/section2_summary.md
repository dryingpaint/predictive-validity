Human genetic evidence linking a program's target to its disease is the clearest preclinical signal of success. Programs whose target had it before they entered the phase reached FDA approval {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs|x2}} times as often as programs without it ({{section2.rs.genetic_any.loa_with|pct}} against {{section2.rs.genetic_any.loa_without|pct}}; 95% CI {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs_hi|x2}}), the likelihood rises with the strength of the evidence, and the association holds at every phase after accounting for disease area, modality, novelty and era. Somatic driver and mouse-model evidence carry weaker signals. Cancer cell-line CRISPR screens single out the same validated oncology targets as somatic drivers and add nothing once those are known, and a target essential in most cell lines, judged by what DepMap had published before entry, is not a clear liability. Yet as a predictor of which individual programs will be approved, the evidence lines raise discrimination only modestly over disease area and modality (held-out AUC {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates.auc|x3}} to {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}}), because most programs have none of them.
