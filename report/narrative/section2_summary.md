Human genetic evidence linking a program's target to its disease is the clearest preclinical signal of success. Programs whose target had it before entering the phase reached approval {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs|x2}} times as often ({{section2.rs.genetic_any.loa_with|pct0}} against {{section2.rs.genetic_any.loa_without|pct0}}; 95% CI {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs_lo|x2}}–{{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs_hi|x2}}), more often the stronger the evidence, and at every phase once disease area, modality, novelty and era are accounted for. Somatic drivers and mouse models carry weaker signals, and a drug potent at its target does better than a weak one. But because most programs have none of this evidence, it predicts which individual programs will be approved only modestly (held-out AUC {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates.auc|x3}} without it, {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}} with it).
