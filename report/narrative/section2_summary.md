Human genetic evidence linking a program's target to its disease is the clearest preclinical signal of success. Programs whose target had it before entering the phase reached approval {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs|x2}} times as often ({{section2.rs.genetic_any.loa_with|pct0}} against {{section2.rs.genetic_any.loa_without|pct0}}), and Phase 2 programs with it advanced {{section3.types.germline.rr|x2}} times as often, more so the stronger the evidence and the closer it lies to the program's own disease. Genes recurrently mutated in the cancer and genetically altered mouse models also predict, though the mouse models mostly ride along with genetic support; the drug's own potency, selectivity and drug-likeness add little, no sharper version of any evidence picks better programs, and an approved drug on the target for a related disease predicts as strongly as genetics. Because most programs have none of this evidence, it sorts individual programs only modestly (held-out AUC {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates.auc|x3}} without it, {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}} with it).
