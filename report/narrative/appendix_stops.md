### How stops were traced and efficacy failures classified

**Tracing the stops the registry does not explain.** Every Phase 1, 2 and 3 program that entered from 2008 to 2025 and stopped was traced ({{stop_causes.traced.stops|int}} Phase 2 and 3 programs, {{stop_causes.traced.phase1|int}} Phase 1); the report's figures use the 2008–2019 entries. Where the registry gives no usable reason, a model read the program's trials (records, posted results, linked papers) and signals of what happened next: whether the drug went on in other diseases, whether its sponsor started any trial afterwards, and whether drugs on the same target stopped at the same time. It then searched the web for company releases, SEC filings, pipeline updates, conference reports and trade news, with ClinicalTrials.gov excluded. It named a cause only where a source supported it, citing only pages its own searches returned, and separately gave a *likely* cause, its best inference from context, where no source says.

**How well the tracing does.** The same procedure was run on {{stop_causes.validation.n|int}} stops whose registry does state a reason, drawn at random across causes, with the reason removed and ClinicalTrials.gov blocked. It named a sourced cause for {{stop_causes.validation.resolved|pct0}} of them, and that cause matched the registry's for {{stop_causes.validation.cause_accuracy_resolved|pct0}} (κ = {{stop_causes.validation.cause_kappa_resolved|x2}}); the likely cause matched for {{stop_causes.validation.likely_accuracy|pct0}} (κ = {{stop_causes.validation.likely_kappa|x2}}). Accuracy differs by cause. Efficacy or exposure stops were recognised {{stop_causes.validation.by_truth.efficacy.likely_correct|pct0}} of the time, and only {{stop_causes.validation.precision_likely.efficacy.correct|pct0}} of the stops it called efficacy or exposure were such stops in the registry: safety stops were often read as efficacy (companies announce both as an unfavourable benefit–risk), and sponsor decisions and operational stops were confused with each other ({{stop_causes.validation.by_truth.sponsor_decision.likely_correct|pct0}} and {{stop_causes.validation.by_truth.operational.likely_correct|pct0}} recognised). The efficacy share after tracing is therefore an upper estimate, and safety is likely under-counted. The registry's stated reason is itself an imperfect reference, since a stated business reason can hide a failed readout. Stops with a stated reason are probably also easier to trace, so accuracy on the unexplained stops is likely lower. One independent check holds: of programs traced to an acquisition, merger, lost funding or closure, {{stop_causes.signals.company_event.sponsor_inactive|pct0}} had a sponsor that started no trial afterwards, against {{stop_causes.signals.efficacy.sponsor_inactive|pct0}} of those traced to efficacy.

**Subcauses.** A second pass, without web search, read each safety, sponsor-decision, operational and positive-readout stop's stated reason or traced source and named the subcause it states or directly implies, or "not stated" where it says no more than the cause. A stop with no traced cause is described by what happened next, from the registry: whether its sponsor started any later trial, and whether the drug was approved or continued in another disease.

**Classifying efficacy failures.** The cohort is every Phase 2 and 3 program that entered in 2008–2019 and stopped for efficacy or for PK, dose or formulation: as the registry records it ({{attribution.by_origin.registry_efficacy.programs|int}} efficacy, {{attribution.by_origin.registry_pk.programs|int}} PK), or as traced with a source from the stops the registry does not explain ({{attribution.by_origin.traced_efficacy.programs|int}} efficacy, {{attribution.by_origin.traced_pk.programs|int}} PK). An exposure failure is the same biology as a failure to engage, so PK stops are broken down here rather than counted apart. For each of the {{attribution.phase23.programs|int}} programs the model would label ({{attribution.unlabelled.phase23|int}} more, mostly gene and cell therapies and live biotherapeutics, were refused), an LLM read an evidence bundle: the failed trial's record, posted results and papers, the drug's other trials and their outcomes, results posted by its Phase 1 trials, its FDA approvals, other drugs on the same target in the same or a related disease, and PubMed abstracts on the drug's human pharmacology and on the drug in that disease. It judged, for humans and at the doses tested, the three pillars of [Morgan et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.drudis.2011.12.020): exposure at the site of action, binding or engagement of the target, and the expected pathway effect. Engagement could also be inferred when the drug worked in another disease through the same target and tissue compartment at a comparable dose. It then said why efficacy failed, using only the bundle and citing the sources it used. The categories and checks were fixed before any program was labelled. Where the bundle held no human evidence of engagement ({{attribution.bundle_only.subcauses.no_engagement_data.share|pct0}} of programs), a second pass, added after the first results, gave the same model the same bundle and the web: it searched full-text papers, conference reports, FDA and EMA reviews and company releases for human pharmacology, and could cite only pages its own searches returned. It decided {{attribution.search.decided|int}} of the {{attribution.search.searched|int}} programs it searched; the figures below use those labels.
