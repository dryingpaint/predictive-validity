Human genetic evidence for the program's own disease is the preclinical evidence that most clearly carried over into Phase 2. Outside oncology, programs with it advanced {{section3.h1.g3.rr|x2}} times as often as programs whose target had no germline genetics ({{section3.h1.g3.ppv|pct0}} against {{section3.h1.g0.ppv|pct0}}); the advantage fades as the evidence moves to related or unrelated diseases, and the same target does better in the diseases its genetics points to (odds ratio {{section3.h2.or|x2}}). Somatic drivers in oncology and animal models also predict, though animal models mostly through their overlap with genetic support. No more specific version of any kind (stronger GWAS, Mendelian evidence, genetics or a mouse model pointing the drug's way) beats the rest of its kind, and an approved drug on the target for a related disease predicts more strongly than any preclinical evidence. Genetic support covered only {{section3.coverage.with_g3|pct0}} of the programs that advanced.
