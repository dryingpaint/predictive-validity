This report rebuilds both questions from primary records. We took every interventional drug or biological study on ClinicalTrials.gov ({{section1.scale.ctgov_studies|int}} studies, snapshot of {{section1.snapshot}}), resolved each intervention to the molecular entity it contains and each condition to a MONDO disease, and joined them to every FDA approval we could document: Drugs@FDA applications, {{section1.scale.fda_approved_uses|int}} approved uses read from 2015–2026 approval letters, current labels, and {{section1.scale.cber_applications|int}} CBER biologics applications rebuilt from the agency's yearly pages and letters. The unit throughout is a *development program*: one molecular entity developed for one indication. An entity counts toward a program only in trials where it is the investigational agent, not a comparator or background therapy.

The report has three parts:

1. **Industry failure modes.** Phase transitions, likelihood of approval (LOA) and the reasons programs stop, for programs that entered each phase in 2015–2025: an updated companion to BIO's *Clinical Development Success Rates 2011–2020*.
2. **Predictive power of preclinical evidence.** For programs aimed at a human protein target, whether the target had human genetic, somatic, animal-model, expression, pathway or functional-screen evidence for the program's disease, as known before the program entered the phase, and how much each line changes the chance of approval.
3. **What translates to the clinic.** Which kinds of evidence, at what strength and in which direction, predicted that a Phase 2 program would advance; on which trial endpoints the advantage shows; and which decision rules applied at Phase 2 entry predicted well enough to call a model, with verified case studies.

Every classification in the pipeline (entity resolution, disease mapping, trial purpose and roles, approvals, stop reasons, trial outcomes, targets, trial endpoints, gene–disease mechanisms and mouse-model relations) was made by an LLM reading the source record and recorded with its prompt and input. Disease mapping, entity resolution, approved uses, program approvals, stop reasons and trial outcomes were also checked against an independent re-derivation of a seeded sample (Appendix); trial purposes and roles were re-decided under a second prompt version and agree with the first on {{quality.retest.trial_purpose|pct0}} and {{quality.retest.trial_moiety_role|pct0}} of trials, and disease mapping, decided twice, chose the same MONDO term for {{quality.retest.indication_mondo|pct0}} of conditions; modality, disease area and targets were not audited, and the Section 3 labels were spot-checked as described there.
