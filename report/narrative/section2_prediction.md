A model on disease area, modality, novelty and entry year alone ranks programs at an AUC of {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates.auc|x3}} on held-out targets; adding every evidence line gives {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}}. Removing human genetic evidence from the full model costs {{section2.ablation.genetic_any.auc_drop|x3}} of AUC (95% CI {{section2.ablation.genetic_any.drop_lo|x3}}–{{section2.ablation.genetic_any.drop_hi|x3}}), a small but real contribution; no other line's removal measurably hurts. The temporal split tells the same story. The gap between a strong relative success and a small gain in discrimination is not a contradiction: genetic support roughly triples the odds for the minority of programs that have it ({{section2.evidence_coverage_ph1.genetic_any|pct0}} of Phase 1 entrants, fewer among the 2008–2016 entrants this model follows), but it says nothing about the majority, so it can sort only a small slice of programs. The draft's AUCs of 0.825 and 0.769 came from a leaked model and phantom programs (AUDIT.md, C33–C34).
