A model on disease area, modality, novelty and entry year alone ranks programs at an AUC of {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates.auc|x3}} on held-out target sets; adding the evidence lines raises it to {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}} (95% CI {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc_lo|x3}}–{{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc_hi|x3}}), and the temporal split agrees ({{section2.prediction.temporal_covariates.auc|x3}} without, {{section2.prediction.temporal_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}} with). Human genetics carries most of the gain: removing it lowers the held-out AUC by {{section2.ablation.genetic_any.auc_drop|x3}} (95% CI {{section2.ablation.genetic_any.drop_lo|x3}}–{{section2.ablation.genetic_any.drop_hi|x3}}), and no other line's removal measurably lowers it. GWAS evidence covered too few of the 2008–2016 entrants to be modelled, so genetics here means rare-variant and Mendelian evidence; CRISPR screens and DepMap essentiality were not published before 2019, so none of these entrants carries them. Discrimination stays modest all the same, and that does not contradict the relative success: genetic support multiplies the chance of approval by about {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs|x2}} for the minority of programs that have it ({{section2.evidence_coverage_ph1.genetic_any|pct0}} of Phase 1 entrants, fewer among the 2008–2016 entrants this model follows), but says nothing about the majority, so it can sort only a small slice of programs.
