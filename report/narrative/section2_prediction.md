A model on disease area, modality, novelty and entry year alone ranks programs at an AUC of {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates.auc|x3}} on held-out targets; adding every evidence line gives {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}} (95% CI {{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc_lo|x3}}–{{section2.prediction.grouped_cv_covariates+evidence.auc_hi|x3}}). No single line's removal measurably lowers that, human genetics included ({{section2.ablation.genetic_any.auc_drop|x3}}, 95% CI {{section2.ablation.genetic_any.drop_lo|x3}}–{{section2.ablation.genetic_any.drop_hi|x3}}): the lines overlap, and what they add they add together. On the temporal split the evidence adds nothing ({{section2.prediction.temporal_covariates.auc|x3}} without, {{section2.prediction.temporal_covariates+evidence.auc|x3}} with). The gap between a strong relative success and so little gain in discrimination is not a contradiction: genetic support multiplies the chance of approval by about {{section2.rs.genetic_any.rs|x2}} for the minority of programs that have it ({{section2.evidence_coverage_ph1.genetic_any|pct0}} of Phase 1 entrants, fewer among the 2008–2016 entrants this model follows), but it says nothing about the majority, so it can sort only a small slice of programs.
