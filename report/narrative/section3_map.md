* **Human genetics.** Mendelian and ClinVar evidence for the disease ({{section3.map.C2.mendelian.rr|x2}}; top-tier curation {{section3.map.C2.mendelian_strong.rr|x2}}) and GWAS ({{section3.map.C2.gwas.rr|x2}}) both carry the signal. Within GWAS, the locus-to-gene score did not grade it: the best score at 0.8 or above gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_strong.rr|x2}}, 0.5–0.8 gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_mid.rr|x2}} and below 0.5 gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_weak.rr|x2}}, each from a few dozen programs. Most GWAS rows in Open Targets are on measurement traits (LDL cholesterol, blood pressure, body-mass index) with no MONDO disease; they count as germline evidence on the target but cannot match a disease, so G3 undercounts genetics found through a trait. Gene burden results start in 2019, too late for these cohorts.
* **Somatic drivers.** In oncology, a somatic driver for the tumour raised the chance of advancing ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.rr|x2}}, {{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.hi|x2}}); requiring the driver's direction to match the drug's did not sharpen it ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic_concordant.rr|x2}}). Germline genetics for the tumour type was the stronger signal ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.g3.rr|x2}}), carried by well-validated targets such as EGFR, ERBB2 and ESR1.
* **Mouse models.** Literature-curated (MGI) mouse models of the disease gave {{section3.map.C2.mgi.rr|x2}} ({{section3.map.C2.mgi.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2.mgi.hi|x2}}), and a top-quartile phenotype match {{section3.map.C2.mgi_strong.rr|x2}}. Open Targets dates MGI models by the gene's earliest literature rather than the model's, so they look older than they are. Knockouts from the IMPC consortia carry no date of their own, so they cannot be frozen at entry and the IMPC rule is untestable.
* **Expression and pathways.** Differential expression in the disease carried nothing ({{section3.map.C2.expression.rr|x2}}; outside oncology {{section3.map.C2_COM.expression.rr|x2}}). Reactome pathway evidence gave {{section3.map.C2.reactome.rr|x2}} from {{section3.map.C2.reactome.n|int}} programs, most of them in hereditary diseases, which Reactome curates. No CRISPR-screen evidence matched a program before its entry.
* **Clinical precedent.** An FDA-approved drug on the target for a related disease (Lin similarity ≥ 0.5) was the strongest single predictor ({{section3.map.C2.b1.rr|x2}}) and was present for {{section3.coverage.with_b1|pct0}} of the programs that advanced.
* **Target liabilities.** Pan-essential targets advanced less often ({{section3.map.C2.pan_essential.rr|x2}}, {{section3.map.C2.pan_essential.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2.pan_essential.hi|x2}}, mostly oncology); loss-of-function intolerance ({{section3.map.C2.constrained.rr|x2}}) and lethality of the mouse knockout ({{section3.map.C2.ko_lethal.rr|x2}}) made no difference.

**Foresight, not hindsight.** Counting all the evidence known today instead of what was known before entry weakens the genetic signal ({{section3.map.C2.g3.today_rr|x2}} against {{section3.map.C2.g3.rr|x2}}): evidence that arrived after entry also arrived for targets that failed.

**What the evidence leaves out.** Of the {{section3.coverage.successes|int}} Phase 2 programs that advanced, {{section3.coverage.with_g3|pct0}} had germline genetics for the disease, {{section3.coverage.with_any_evidence|pct0}} had any of the disease-specific evidence families, and {{section3.coverage.neither|pct0}} had neither such evidence nor an approved drug on the target for a related disease.
