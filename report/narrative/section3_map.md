* **Human genetics.** Mendelian and ClinVar evidence for the disease ({{section3.map.C2.mendelian.rr|x2}}; top-tier curation {{section3.map.C2.mendelian_strong.rr|x2}}) carries the signal; GWAS ({{section3.map.C2.gwas.rr|x2}}) points the same way but not clearly. Within GWAS, the locus-to-gene score did not grade it: the best score at 0.8 or above gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_strong.rr|x2}}, 0.5–0.8 gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_mid.rr|x2}} and below 0.5 gives {{section3.map.C2.gwas_weak.rr|x2}}, each from a few dozen programs. Most GWAS rows in Open Targets are on measurement traits (LDL cholesterol, blood pressure, body-mass index) with no MONDO disease; they count as germline evidence on the target but cannot match a disease, so genetics for the disease is undercounted when it was found through a trait. Gene burden results start in 2019, too late for these cohorts.
* **Tumour genetics.** In oncology, a gene recurrently mutated in the cancer raised the chance of advancing ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.rr|x2}}, {{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic.hi|x2}}); requiring the mutation to match the drug did not sharpen it ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.somatic_concordant.rr|x2}}). Germline genetics for the tumour type was the stronger signal ({{section3.map.C2_ONC.g3.rr|x2}}), carried by well-validated targets such as EGFR, ERBB2 and ESR1.
* **Genetically altered mouse models.** Literature-curated (MGI) mouse models of the disease gave {{section3.map.C2.mgi.rr|x2}} ({{section3.map.C2.mgi.lo|x2}}–{{section3.map.C2.mgi.hi|x2}}), and a top-quartile phenotype match {{section3.map.C2.mgi_strong.rr|x2}}. Each MGI model is dated by its first paper, and each knockout from the IMPC consortia by the first IMPC data release with calls for it (2015 at the earliest).
* **Human diseased tissue and curated mechanisms.** A change in the target's RNA level in diseased tissue carried nothing ({{section3.map.C2.expression.rr|x2}}; outside oncology {{section3.map.C2_COM.expression.rr|x2}}). A curated disease mechanism (Reactome) gave {{section3.map.C2.reactome.rr|x2}} from {{section3.map.C2.reactome.n|int}} programs, most of them in hereditary diseases, which Reactome curates.
* **Clinical precedent.** An FDA-approved drug on the target for a related disease (Lin similarity ≥ 0.5) was stronger than any broad kind of preclinical evidence ({{section3.map.C2.b1.rr|x2}}) and was present for {{section3.coverage.with_b1|pct0}} of the programs that advanced.
* **Safety risk of the target.** Loss-of-function intolerance ({{section3.map.C2.constrained.rr|x2}}) and lethality of the mouse knockout ({{section3.map.C2.ko_lethal.rr|x2}}) made no difference. Essentiality across cell lines could not be known before entry for any program in the window (above).

**What the evidence leaves out.** Of the {{section3.coverage.successes|int}} Phase 2 programs that advanced, {{section3.coverage.with_g3|pct0}} had germline genetics for the disease, {{section3.coverage.with_any_evidence|pct0}} had any of the disease-specific evidence families, and {{section3.coverage.neither|pct0}} had neither such evidence nor an approved drug on the target for a related disease.
