### What first proposed the target

For every target–disease pair whose first Phase 2 or 3 trial started in 2008–2019, an LLM searched the literature published before that trial for what first linked the target to the disease, and two further checks confirmed each answer against its cited source (appendix). A cited source dated before entry settles the origin of {{target_origin.origins.settled|int}} of the {{target_origin.origins.labelled|int}} pairs.

![What first proposed the target for the disease, before its first Phase 2 or 3 trial](report/output/figures/target_origin_share.png)

*Target–disease pairs first tested in Phase 2 or 3 in 2008–2019 whose origin a cited source dated before entry settles ({{target_origin.origins.settled|int}} of {{target_origin.origins.labelled|int}}); each share is of those {{target_origin.origins.settled|int}}. Of the rest, {{target_origin.origins.unclear|int}} are unclear, {{target_origin.origins.disputed|int}} are disputed between the two checkers and {{target_origin.origins.not_reached|int}} could not be labelled.*

**Few targets were first proposed by genetics.** Genetics of any kind, inherited human variants, the tumour's own mutations or mouse genetics, first proposed {{target_origin.origins.genetic_share|pct0}} of settled pairs. Most targets came from a change seen in diseased tissue ({{target_origin.origins.share.diseased_tissue|pct0}}), a drug-like change in an animal model of the disease ({{target_origin.origins.share.animal_model_pharmacology|pct0}}) or an earlier drug on the target approved for another disease ({{target_origin.origins.share.earlier_drug_other_disease|pct0}}), followed by clinical observation ({{target_origin.origins.share.clinical_observation|pct0}}) and work in primary human cells ({{target_origin.origins.share.human_cell_perturbation|pct0}}).

**Targets first proposed by a biomarker or a tissue change may stop more often as "not causal", but this was not confirmed.** One test was registered before it was run: entries whose target was first proposed by an observational biomarker or a diseased-tissue change stop because the target was not causal more often than entries whose target was first proposed by human genetics. They did so {{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.rr|x2}} times as often per entry ({{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.exposed.rate|pct0}} against {{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.reference.rate|pct0}}), the direction registered, but the 95% interval ({{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.lo|x2}}–{{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.hi|x2}}) includes 1: human genetics first proposed few targets, and only {{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.reference.events|int}} of its {{target_origin.tests.primary.o1.reference.entries|int}} entries stopped this way. No registered sensitivity analysis changes the conclusion (appendix).

Most "not causal" calls rest on the target never later working in the disease rather than on a trial that showed it, so the comparison is largely about which origins lead to targets that never succeed; an earlier drug approved for another disease is shown apart, because that approval is the record that labels most of its stops "not causal".

**Targets chosen from genetics were approved about twice as often.** Programs whose target was first proposed by human or tumour genetics reached approval {{target_origin.routes.genetics.approved.rr|x2}} times as often as programs whose target came by any other settled route ({{target_origin.routes.genetics.approved.lo|x2}}–{{target_origin.routes.genetics.approved.hi|x2}}), and fewer of them stopped because the target was not causal ({{target_origin.routes.genetics.mode_not_causal.rr|x2}}×), though that difference is not significant ({{target_origin.routes.genetics.mode_not_causal.lo|x2}}–{{target_origin.routes.genetics.mode_not_causal.hi|x2}}).
