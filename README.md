# Predictive Validity

**Which lines of preclinical evidence actually predict clinical success?**

About 92% of drugs that enter clinical trials fail, and the cost of bringing a drug to market keeps rising. We know little about *why* most of those trials fail. Jack Scannell named the problem *predictive validity*: drug discovery stalls when the models and evidence used to pick targets fail to predict what happens in patients.

Over the past 60 years the industry has added new model organisms, large-scale in vitro and in silico screening, and large compilations of omics data. This project asks which of these lines of evidence have actually improved clinical success rates.

We analyze the 28,193 clinical trials that started after 2015 and ended before 2026. The work has two parts:

1. **Industry failure modes.** We break trial failures down by clinical phase, therapeutic modality and disease indication. This part works as an updated companion to the BIO report on clinical trial failures from 2010 to 2020.
2. **Clinical predictiveness of preclinical evidence.** We compile the standard preclinical lines of evidence (genetic precedent, cell pathway validation, in vivo evidence, and others) from public databases and from classifying the published literature. We then measure how well each one predicts the failure modes above.
