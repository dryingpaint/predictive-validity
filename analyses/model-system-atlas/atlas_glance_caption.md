**Figure. Did the preclinical models predict what happened in patients?**
**a**, For each of the four questions a preclinical model is asked, the headline status of the cell across the 30 drafted tiles. A tile is one mechanism (target, action) in one disease, tracked when at least three programs entered Phase 2 for it in 2008–2019; the number after the mechanism is its programs. Every model system compared with patients for the class is a track; a cell's headline is the best latest status among its tracks that predicted before the patient result, never compared when none did, and a retrodiction never sets it (rules v3, decision 6). Matched: a published comparison showed the model's prediction held in patients. Contradicted: it failed. A dose failure hatched "through efficacy" is a drug that reached its exposure target and still failed, the efficacy failure seen from the dose cell, which the rules count once. **b**, The same 120 cells, one row per tile grouped by disease area. The mark is the headline status, the bold year is when it was earned, then the model that set it. A contradicted cell carries, on a second line, why it failed. A small filled square marks a matched cell in which another model's track ended contradicted, with that track's finding; a small open square marks an efficacy cell whose surrogate matched while the outcome trial was negative. Statuses were drafted and checked by separate agents and adjudicated; when a cell is split by drug modality the worst strip is shown. Timelines, quotes and sources are in the interactive atlas. Source: `preclin.atlas_track_entry` and `atlas_track_verdict`; drafts in `analyses/model-system-atlas/drafts/v3/`.
